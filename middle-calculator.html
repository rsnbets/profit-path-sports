Middle Calculator

Two books, two different numbers — and a gap in between. Bet over at one and under at the other; if the result lands in the gap, both tickets cash. It looks free. It usually isn't — this shows the real worst case, best case, and break-even.

In plain English

Two sportsbooks posted different numbers for the same game. You bet both sides — and if the final result lands in the gap between them, both bets win. This tool shows you whether that gap is actually worth chasing.

Middle Size the middle Bet both sides at different numbers — see your worst case, your best case, and whether it's actually worth it.
What's a middle?
Two books disagree on the number for the same market. You bet the over at one and the under at the other, far enough apart that the result can land between them — the "middle." If it does, both tickets cash. If it misses, you only lose the juice on the losing side. Example: Over 47.5 at one book, Under 50.5 at another — a final total of 48, 49, or 50 wins both.

★ Key Point — Most Middles Are −EV
A middle feels free — you only drop a little juice when it misses. But it misses most of the time, and that juice adds up. The bet is only +EV if the gap lands often enough to clear the break-even hit rate above. Realistic numbers are low: even a real 3-point NFL total middle around the closing line only lands maybe 6–9% of the time, and clustered or narrow middles are far thinner. So the honest test is simple — if your real middle-hit probability beats break-even, bet it; if it doesn't, or you can't estimate it, you're gambling, not middling. The middles deep dive →
How to use this

What a middle is. Two sportsbooks post different numbers for the same market. You take the over at the book with the lower number and the under at the book with the higher number. If the final result lands between the two numbers, both bets win — you've "middled" it. If it lands outside, only one wins and you lose the juice on the other.

How to use it.

  • Enter Side A — your stake and the odds at the first book (e.g. Over 47.5 at −110).
  • Enter Side B — the odds on the other side at the second book. Leave sizing on Auto and the calculator sets the Side B stake so your loss is equal whichever way the middle misses.
  • Read The Numbers — worst case (the gap misses), best case (it hits, both cash), and the risk-to-reward ratio.

The test that matters — break-even. Add a middle-hit probability and the calculator returns expected value and the break-even hit rate — how often the gap must land for the bet to be +EV. If your honest estimate of the hit rate doesn't beat break-even, the middle is −EV, however fun the upside looks.

Realistic middle probabilities. Don't guess — pull the number from a sharp book's price on the exact result range. As rough anchors: a genuine 3-point NFL total middle around the closing line lands roughly 6–9% of the time, but the same gap width on a clustered distribution (spreads, low-scoring sports, quarter totals) can be far thinner — width is structural, probability is empirical. Wide gaps from a real line move are where genuine middles live. The middles deep dive breaks down which sports have smooth vs. clustered score distributions.
