Conference spots are the big ones

Half this product is college prospects with no NFL team, so breaks sell them as conference spots. SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 and ACC take about 43% of the break between them — a single SEC spot is worth roughly five NFL team spots and eighteen Packers spots.

Autos carry it

926 of the 2,139 cards in the checklist are autographs, and they are spread far more evenly than the base sets. That flattens the NFL spots: the gap from the best NFL team to the worst is about 3×, where a baseball board runs 25×. Most NFL spots are closer in value than they look.

Build any spot

Breakers split these up by team, by conference, by custom bundle, sometimes by single player. Tick a spot, a whole group with its header box, or open a spot and tick individual players — the panel prices whatever you pick as one spot. Ticking a player takes every card they have, including cards filed under another spot: Joe Burrow brings his LSU cards with his Bengals ones. Overlapping picks are counted once, never twice. Your selection is remembered on this device.

Set the breaker total

Add up every spot the breaker is selling and type that number in — the whole board rescales to it. The $3,000 default is a placeholder, not a market price. For auctioned randoms, bid against share: a conference spot at NFL-team money is the clearest value in this product.

Shares are card counts from the official Topps checklist, weighted base 1 / insert 3 / auto 25. Raw and weighted shares agree within 0.4 points, so the ranking is not sensitive to those weights. Unlike the Cosmic Chrome board, these are not adjusted for prospect hype. Conference mapping uses 2026 membership — Oregon and Washington are Big Ten, Cal and Stanford are ACC, and the rebuilt Pac-12 sits in All Others along with Notre Dame. The box configuration selector changes which sets are in the pool: Mega drops twelve insert and autograph sets and adds two of its own, Value drops the multi-player autographs. Hobby, FDI, Breaker and Jumbo share an identical card pool and differ only in pull rates. One caveat: Topps' odds sheet lists the three paper autograph sets (450 cards) only as Value-exclusive colour parallels with no base row, which is almost certainly a listing quirk rather than a real Value exclusive, so they are counted in every configuration. Treating them as Value-only would move NFL from 51.2% to 46.4%.
