CLV

You can place a hundred +EV bets and still be down for the month. Closing line value is the scoreboard that tells you the truth before the money does. Most bettors track profit. The ones who track CLV know whether that profit will last.

What it is

You can place a hundred +EV bets and still be down 8 units after a month. Variance is real. So how do you actually know you're a winning bettor — and not just running hot or running cold?

CLV. Closing Line Value.

The closing line is the price the market lands on right before tip-off. It's the sharpest, most-informed number the entire market could produce — every sharp bettor, every model, every piece of late-breaking news baked in. By the time the line closes, almost all the noise has been squeezed out.

If you bet a line and it moves toward you by the time it closes — meaning you locked in a price the closing market now considers too generous — you got positive CLV. You beat the close. Beating the close, over and over, is the only reliable proof that you're +EV.

Why this actually works (in plain English)

The Concert-Ticket Resale

You buy a concert ticket three months out for $50. The show looks like it'll be popular, but demand is still uncertain — that's why the price is low.

Show day arrives. Resale tickets are going for $80. Demand ran higher than expected; the market closed at $80.

You paid $50 for something the market now values at $80. You got a great deal — and that's true whether or not you actually go. Don't show up, you resell for $80. Do show up, you got into a now-$80 event for $50. Either way the math wins, because you bought below where the market closed.

That's CLV. The "closing line" is the final price the market settles on right before the event starts. If your bet's price was better than the closing price, you beat the close. Over many bets, consistently beating the close is the only proof you're +EV. One bet's outcome is noise. Beating the close 100+ times is signal.

In the analogyIn real betting
Buying the ticket at $50Placing your bet at the price you got
Resale market closing at $80The closing line
The $30 gap in your favorYour CLV
Going to the show, or notThe bet result — irrelevant to whether the price was good

The key insight: the price you paid was a great deal the moment you paid it, regardless of how the show turned out. Bets work the same way. A +EV bet is +EV at placement; CLV is the receipt that proves it — and the only metric that survives variance.

The math behind it

You bet Mavs +6.5 at −110. By tip-off the line has moved to Mavs +5.5 — the closing market now pays less for the side you took, so your +6.5 was the better number. Devig the close to find the fair price the market thinks +6.5 is worth — say −125 — and compare:

Your CLV on this bet Price you got     :  −110  (decimal 1.91)
Fair price at close:  −125  (decimal 1.80)
──────────────────────────────────
CLV  =  ( 1.91 ÷ 1.80 ) − 1  =  +6.1%

The bet itself might still lose — the Mavs could cover by 1, or not at all. CLV doesn't care. It measures the quality of the price you got, not the outcome of the game.

Why it matters more than you think

A bettor running +5% CLV over 200 bets is almost certainly a winning bettor — even if they're sitting at −3 units on the year. Variance just hasn't caught up yet.

A bettor running −2% CLV over 200 bets is almost certainly a losing bettor — even if they're sitting at +12 units on the year. They got lucky. Variance always catches up.

CLV is the only short-term metric that doesn't lie. Profit is too noisy. Win rate is too noisy. CLV is signal — at the cost of being a little less satisfying than counting your winnings on a hot week.

What sharps do about it

They review every bet at close. They track CLV obsessively, not unit profit. Positive CLV — they keep doing exactly what they're doing, even through losing weeks. CLV turns negative — they investigate before blaming bad luck.

You can't always win. You can always check whether you're picking sharper than the market — and that's the only thing that tells you whether winning is on the way.

★ Key Point — CLV Doesn't Lie, Your Bankroll Does
Over a few hundred bets, profit is mostly variance — you can be a genuinely winning bettor sitting at a loss, or a losing one sitting at a profit. CLV cuts straight through it: it measures whether you're consistently buying a better price than the sharpest number the market produced. Positive CLV over 200+ bets means you're +EV and the money is coming; negative CLV means you're not and the money is going — no matter what this week's scoreboard says. Track CLV, not last week's profit. It's the same lesson as +EV — judge the process, not the result — with CLV as the process made measurable.
