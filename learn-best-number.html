Get the Best Number

Two bettors make the exact same 1,000 bets. One wins. One loses. Picks don't matter. Numbers do. The difference is ten cents of price — and it's the easiest edge in betting to take back.

The capper who wins while you lose

Say you pay for picks from a genuinely winning bettor — no scam, no fake record. Long-run, his bets hit 53%, and at his prices that's a real edge.

He bets his plays the moment he finds them, shopping several books, averaging −105. By the time the play hits your phone and you fire it at your one sportsbook, the line has moved and you're laying −115. Same bets. Same games. Same 53%.

Him — 1,000 bets at −105, 53% win rate 530 wins  × +1.00u  =  +530.0u
470 losses × −1.05u  =  −493.5u
──────────────────────────
Net             =  +36.5u
You — the SAME 1,000 bets at −115, same 53% 530 wins  × +1.00u  =  +530.0u
470 losses × −1.15u  =  −540.5u
──────────────────────────
Net             =  −10.5u

He made 36.5 units. You lost 10.5 — on identical picks. Ten cents of price is the entire difference between a winning bettor and a losing one. And that's before you count what you paid him for the picks.

"If it wins, it wins… right?" NO.

Here's the trap — especially when you're following someone's picks. The bet cashes and you think: "the capper had −105, I had −115, and we both won. The price cost me nothing."

Look at what each ticket actually pays:

A winning ticket hides the price — a losing one shows it Win at −105  =  +1.00u   |   Win at −115  =  +1.00u  identical
Lose at −105  =  −1.05u   |   Lose at −115  =  −1.15u  there's the leak

Sized this way — betting to win one unit, like the capper math above — the wins pay the same and the worse number only bills you on the losses. And you were always going to have losses: 470 of them, in the math above. Ten extra cents on 470 losing tickets is the entire 47-unit gap between his +36.5 and your −10.5.

Prefer betting a flat $100 instead? The leak doesn't disappear — it just moves to the wins: your winning ticket pays $86.96 at −115 while his pays $95.24 at −105. Same ten cents, different hiding spot. However you stake, the cost per ticket is small enough that you never feel it — which is exactly why losing bettors never fix it.

A win at a bad number is a mistake that happened to pay off — like running a red light and making it through. The result was fine; the decision was still costing you. Winning bettors don't grade bets by whether they cashed. They grade them by the number they got against fair — because over a thousand tickets, that's the only grade that pays.

Where your ten cents went

Nothing shady happened. Three ordinary things ate your price:

  • The line moved — because of him. When a popular capper releases a play, his subscribers are the line move. Every follower who bets before you makes your number worse. You're not copying his bet; you're buying what his bet already sold.
  • He shopped. You didn't. He checked several books and took the best price on the board. You took whatever your one app happened to be showing.
  • He bet at 9am. You bet at lunch. Delay costs cents, and as the math above shows, cents are the whole game.

The price you get isn't a detail of the bet. It is the bet.

Line shopping — the easiest edge in betting

The same bet is almost never the same price everywhere. Here's what each price demands of you just to break even:

Break-even win rate by price — same bet, different books −105  →  51.2%
−110  →  52.4%
−115  →  53.5%
−120  →  54.5%

From −105 to −120 is 3.3 points of win rate — on the identical bet. No model, no insight, no picks. Just checking more than one book before you click. That's why winning bettors run accounts at many books: not for the promos, but so every bet gets the best number on the board.

Remember the vig lesson: the book builds a markup into every line. Line shopping is how you claw part of it back — every cent of price you gain is vig you didn't pay.

What winning bettors do about it

Winning bettors treat picks — their own included — as raw material. The decision to bet is made by the number:

  • Know the fair price first. Devig the market (Devig Calculator) so you know what the bet is actually worth.
  • Shop every bet. Compare the boards and take the best number — not the convenient app.
  • Pass when the number's gone. If the price has moved past fair, the edge left before you arrived. A good pick at a bad number is a bad bet.
★ Key Point — The Pick Is Never the Edge
A pick has no value by itself — its entire value lives in the price you get relative to the fair price. The same pick can be a great bet at −105 and a losing bet at −115, as the capper math above proves. So never ask "who should I bet?" Ask "is this number better than fair?" — and this doctrine has a scoreboard: CLV (Lesson 06) measures whether the numbers you're getting beat the market's final answer.
