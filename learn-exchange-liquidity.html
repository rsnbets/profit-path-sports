Exchanges 101

ProphetX, Novig, Kalshi and Polymarket, explained from zero. Percent odds, fees, and how to read the screen.

What you'll have when you're done

  • How to read percent odds without converting in your head every time.
  • What each of the four exchanges is, and how they differ.
  • What every bet really costs once fees are in. They are not all the same.
  • How to read the order screen: best price, money available, and who is on the other side.
  • A first-trade checklist so your first bet goes the way you meant it to.
01

What changed in 2026

A year ago, most US exchanges ran as sweepstakes apps or state-licensed books. That is over. ProphetX and Novig are now federally regulated exchanges, the same kind of license Kalshi and Polymarket US hold. The regulator is the CFTC, the agency that oversees futures markets.

That license came with a rule most bettors noticed right away. In August 2026 the CFTC told regulated exchanges to stop showing American odds. Its staff wrote that plus and minus odds were "likely to mislead market participants about the nature of the transaction." By September the apps had switched. You now see percentages.

Every exchange price is now a percentage. 48% is the price. It is not a confidence rating and it is not a prediction from the app.

If you only learn one thing from this guide, learn to read that number fast.

Two other changes matter. Fees moved around this fall, and state access is in flux. Several states have sued or ordered exchanges out. This guide names fees and dates. It does not list states, because that list changes week to week. Check the app before you deposit.

Facts on this page checked October 24, 2026.

02

Percent odds, first

An exchange position is a contract. It pays a fixed amount if your side wins and nothing if it loses. The percentage is the share of that payout you pay up front.

  • 48% means you pay 48 cents for a contract that pays $1. You profit 52 cents if it wins.
  • Lower percent, bigger payout. Under 50% is an underdog. Over 50% is a favorite.
  • 50% is even money. +100 in the old format.

Three shortcuts

  • Near 50%, one point is about 4 cents of American odds. 51% is −104. 52% is about −108. 52.4% is the classic −110.
  • The two sides add up to a little over 100%. The amount over 100 is your cost to trade, called the spread. A standard −110 / −110 sportsbook market adds to 104.8%. A busy exchange market is often 101% to 102%.
  • The displayed percent does not include fees. Fees are charged on top, and they differ by venue. Section 04 has the math.

03

The four venues

All four are CFTC-regulated and all four show percent prices. They differ in who is on the other side, what they charge, and how deep the markets run.

ProphetX

Sports exchange

A peer-to-peer order book built for sports. Approved as an exchange June 11, 2026 and live nationwide June 18. Independent market makers add depth on the big markets.

Fee
2% of net winnings on straight bets. Nothing on losses. 0% on parlays.
Depth
Real on headline games. Thin on niche props. Weekly volume ran far below Novig's in October.
Know
The fee is on profit, so you pay nothing on a losing bet.

Novig

Sports exchange

Operates through Ludlow Exchange, approved June 16, 2026. Dropped its sweepstakes product and relaunched with real money August 4.

Fee
Pregame takers pay nothing. Live takers pay 0.06 × P × (1 − P) per contract since October 15. Makers pay nothing and earn credits.
Depth
About ten times ProphetX's weekly volume in early October, and much more money on the screen in our own pulls.
Know
Novig adds its own liquidity when a market is thin. Some of the money on the screen is the venue, not outside bettors.

Kalshi

Prediction market

The best-known prediction market. Sports sit next to politics, economics and weather. Prices run from 1¢ to 99¢ per contract.

Fee
Takers: 0.07 × contracts × P × (1 − P), rounded up to the cent. Makers on sports game series: 0.0175 on the same formula.
Depth
Deep on major game lines. Varies a lot on props.
Know
Kalshi is the main target of state lawsuits. Access in your state can change on short notice.

Polymarket US

Prediction market

A separate US app, not the global crypto site. Full ID check before you deposit. Prices show in cents and percent.

Fee
Takers: Θ × contracts × P × (1 − P). Θ was 0.05 for sports this summer and about 0.07 by mid-September. Makers pay nothing and can earn rebates.
Depth
Close to sportsbook prices on main lines. Props and parlays run thin.
Know
Polymarket changes fee rates by category often. Check the app before you size up.

The short version

  • Betting pregame and taking a posted price? Novig costs nothing extra.
  • Betting live? ProphetX's 2% of winnings is the cheapest of the four for a taker.
  • Want to post your own price? Novig and Polymarket US charge makers nothing. Kalshi charges makers a small fee on sports games.
  • Want the most money to bet into? Between the two sports exchanges, Novig usually has more. Kalshi and Polymarket US can be deep on main lines. Check market by market.

Fee rates from each venue's published schedule or help center, as reported through October 2026. ProphetX's rate comes from its help center as reprinted October 2. Polymarket US's current sports rate comes from a September 17 review. Confirm both in the app.

04

What a bet really costs

Fees come in two shapes.

  • A cut of your profit (ProphetX). You pay 2% of what you win. A losing bet costs nothing extra.
  • A fee on the price (Novig live, Kalshi, Polymarket US). The formula is a rate × P × (1 − P). That is largest at 50% and shrinks toward the ends. You pay it when the trade fills, win or lose.

Here is the same bet at 55% (−122) on every venue, with the fee folded in. The all-in percent is the number to compare with a sportsbook.

Kalshi rounds each order's fee up to the cent, so small orders cost slightly more than shown.

The spread between the cheapest and the most expensive row is almost two points, or about nine cents of American odds. That is often bigger than the edge you are trying to capture. Fees decide which venue you bet at, and sometimes whether to bet at all.

Rule of thumb: on a fee-on-price venue, taking a near 50/50 price costs you about 1.5 to 1.75 points. Posting your own price on Novig or Polymarket US costs nothing. That is the main reason sharp bettors make instead of take.
Do this math once per bet, not in your head. Pick the venue, maker or taker, and pregame or live. The calculator gives the all-in price.

05

Reading the order screen

Every bet on an exchange was posted by another user before you could take it. The screen is a queue of those posted bets, best price first. Here is a typical total, laid out the way most apps show it:

Total 44.5 · money available to bet each side
Over 44.5
48%$16,240
49%$4,100
50%$2,500
Under 44.5
53%$1,150
54%$3,300
55%$900
Spread: 48 + 53 = 101%Matched: $212,400
What each number means
Over 48% · $16,240The best price to bet the Over right now. You can bet up to $16,240 there.
49%, 50%The next prices in line on the Over. They cost more, so you only reach them once the 48% money is gone. A bet bigger than $16,240 walks up the queue to worse prices.
Under 53% · $1,150The best price to bet the Under, with only $1,150 available there.
Spread 101%48 + 53. The one point over 100 is what a taker pays to cross the market, before fees.
Matched $212,400Bets already filled on this market. It shows how active the market is. It is history, not an open offer.
Which is the best price? For you as a bettor, a lower percent is always better. The app lists the best available price first on each side. Everything below it in the queue costs more.
06

Who is on the other side

The $16,240 available on the Over did not come from the app. Someone posted it. To offer you the Over, they had to bet the Under. So:

Money available on one side means makers are on the other side.

$16,240 available on the Over at 48% means makers are on the Under for that amount, at 52%.

Percent odds made this easier. The old rule was "flip the side, flip the sign." The new rule is flip the side, subtract from 100.

Flip the side, subtract from 100
ScreenOver 48% · $16,240 available
MakersOn the Under at 52%
ScreenUnder 53% · $1,150 available
MakersOn the Over at 47%

This screen has a lot more maker money on the Under than on the Over. That lopsided book is the first thing Part 2 teaches you to read. It is a clue about where informed money sits. It is not proof, and Part 2 covers why.

One more use of the flip: the makers' price is a ceiling. If a sportsbook offers the Under below 52% (better than −108), it is paying more than the people who posted $16,240 were willing to accept. That comparison is the core of cross-market betting.

07

Your first trade

Take or make

  • Take a posted price and the bet fills right away. You pay the spread and any taker fee.
  • Make by posting your own price, and you wait for someone to take it. It may never fill. If it does, you skip the spread and, on most venues, the fee.

The checklist

  • Check the venue is open in your state before you deposit. Age rules vary from 18 to 21 by venue and state.
  • Convert the price to American odds if you are comparing with a sportsbook.
  • Add the fee for your venue, role and timing. Use the all-in price, not the screen price.
  • Check the money available at your price. If your bet is bigger than the top level, part of it fills at worse prices.
  • Read the settlement rules for props and overtime. Contracts settle on their written rules, and some have no push.
  • Cancel posted orders you no longer want. A resting order can fill hours later, after the news has changed.
  • Log the price you got and the closing price. Beating the close is the honest measure of whether you bet well.
  • Exchange prices are percentages. Lower is better for you.
  • The screen price leaves out the fee. Compare all-in prices.
  • Money available on one side means makers are on the other side. Flip the side, subtract from 100.
  • Novig pregame is free to take. ProphetX's 2% of winnings is the cheapest live take. Making is free on Novig and Polymarket US.
