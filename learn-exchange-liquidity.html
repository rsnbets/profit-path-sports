Exchanges 101

ProphetX, Novig, Kalshi and Polymarket, explained from zero. Percent odds, the order book, and taking a price or making one.

What you'll have when you're done

  • How an exchange works, and how it earns without betting against you.
  • How to read percent odds without converting in your head every time.
  • How to read the order book: best price, money available, and who is on the other side.
  • Taker or maker: what each costs and when each makes sense.
  • What every bet really costs once the spread and the fee are in.
  • The four venues side by side, each with its own walkthrough.
01

You bet against other people, not the house

At a sportsbook, the book sets the price and takes the other side of your bet. On an exchange, every bet was posted by another user before you could take it. The exchange matches the two of you and holds the money until the game settles.

The exchange earns a fee on the trade. It does not win when you lose. Exchanges do not limit winning bettors. They earn fees either way.

A year ago, most US exchanges ran as sweepstakes apps or state-licensed books. That is over. ProphetX and Novig are now federally regulated exchanges, the same kind of license Kalshi and Polymarket US hold. The regulator is the CFTC, the agency that oversees futures markets.

02

Price as a percent

That license came with a rule most bettors noticed right away. In August 2026 the CFTC told regulated exchanges to stop showing American odds. Its staff wrote that plus and minus odds were "likely to mislead market participants about the nature of the transaction." By September the apps had switched. You now see percentages.

Every exchange price is now a percentage. 48% is the price. It is not a confidence rating and it is not a prediction from the app.

If you only learn one thing from this guide, learn to read that number fast.

An exchange position is a contract. It pays a fixed amount if your side wins and nothing if it loses. The percentage is the share of that payout you pay up front.

  • 48% means you pay 48 cents for a contract that pays $1. You profit 52 cents if it wins.
  • Lower percent, bigger payout. Under 50% is an underdog. Over 50% is a favorite.
  • 50% is even money. +100 in the old format.

Three shortcuts

  • Near 50%, one point is about 4 cents of American odds. 51% is −104. 52% is about −108. 52.4% is the classic −110.
  • The two sides add up to a little over 100%. The amount over 100 is your cost to trade, called the spread. A standard −110 / −110 sportsbook market adds to 104.8%. A busy exchange market is often 101% to 102%.
  • The displayed percent does not include fees. Fees are charged on top, and they differ by venue. Section 05 has the math.

Sportsbooks still post American odds. So any comparison between an exchange and FanDuel or DraftKings means converting one side. Do it the same way every time. Most mistakes on exchanges are conversion mistakes.

03

The order book

Every bet on an exchange was posted by another user before you could take it. The screen is a queue of those posted bets, best price first. Here is a typical total, laid out the way most apps show it:

What each number means
Over 48% · $16,240The best price to bet the Over right now. You can bet up to $16,240 there.
49%, 50%The next prices in line on the Over. They cost more, so you only reach them once the 48% money is gone. A bet bigger than $16,240 walks up the queue to worse prices.
Under 53% · $1,150The best price to bet the Under, with only $1,150 available there.
Spread 101%48 + 53. The one point over 100 is what a taker pays to cross the market, before fees.
Matched $212,400Bets already filled on this market. It shows how active the market is. It is history, not an open offer.
Which is the best price? For you as a bettor, a lower percent is always better. The app lists the best available price first on each side. Everything below it in the queue costs more.

Who is on the other side

The $16,240 available on the Over did not come from the app. Someone posted it. To offer you the Over, they had to bet the Under. So:

Money available on one side means makers are on the other side.

$16,240 available on the Over at 48% means makers are on the Under for that amount, at 52%.

Percent odds made this easier. The old rule was "flip the side, flip the sign." The new rule is flip the side, subtract from 100.

Flip the side, subtract from 100
ScreenOver 48% · $16,240 available
MakersOn the Under at 52%
ScreenUnder 53% · $1,150 available
MakersOn the Over at 47%

This screen has a lot more maker money on the Under than on the Over. That lopsided book is the first thing Reading the book teaches you to read. It is a clue about where informed money sits. It is not proof, and that page covers why.

One more use of the flip: the makers' price is a ceiling. If a sportsbook offers the Under below 52% (better than −108), it is paying more than the people who posted $16,240 were willing to accept. That comparison is the core of cross-market betting.

The trading words

Some apps and most articles use trading words for the same screen.

  • Ask (or offer): a posted price you can take. Over 48% with $16,240 available is the best ask on the Over.
  • Bid: a price someone has posted to buy a side. The makers behind that $16,240 are bidding 52% for the Under. One order, seen from each side.
  • Spread: the gap between the two best prices. 48 + 53 = 101%, so the spread is one point.
  • Depth: the money available at each price.
04

Taker or maker

Every exchange bet has two roles. One person posted the price. The other took it. You choose which one you are on every bet.

  • Take a posted price and the bet fills right away. You pay the spread and any taker fee.
  • Make by posting your own price, and you wait for someone to take it. It may never fill. If it does, you skip the spread and, on most venues, the fee.

What you doTake a price already on the screenPost your own price and wait

SpreadYou pay itYou skip it
FeeThe venue's taker feeNothing on Novig and Polymarket US. Small on Kalshi sports games. 2% of winnings on ProphetX either way

What making looks like

In a wide market, the gap between the two best prices is money left on the table. A taker pays it. A maker can collect it.

Player hits Over 0.5
ExchangeOver available at 60%. Under available at 48%. Sum 108%, so the market is wide.
SportsbooksDe-vigged across books, fair on the Over is about 57%.
PostPost an order to bet the Over at 55%. To a taker it shows as the Under available at 45%.
If it fillsYou hold the Over at 55% against a 57% fair. On Novig or Polymarket US, no fee.

Fill risk

  • It may never fill. That costs you nothing but the bet you didn't make.
  • It can fill in part. Post $100 and a taker who wants $40 leaves you with a $40 bet and $60 still waiting. Cancel the rest or leave it up.
  • Fills come at bad times. Your order is most likely to get taken right when news turns against you. A lineup change can make your 55% a gift to the taker.
  • So manage the order. Post closer to game time, watch the news, and cancel when anything changes. Don't leave orders sitting overnight.
  • Your fair price has to come from somewhere else. Use the sportsbook consensus, not the wide exchange midpoint. Posting at the exchange's own midpoint just gets you a coin flip.

Takers have a fill risk too. A bet bigger than the money at the best price walks up the queue to worse prices.

When each makes sense

  • Take when the market is tight and the price is good right now. At 101% to 102% there is little spread left to save by waiting.
  • Take when the fee is zero. Novig charges pregame takers nothing.
  • Make when the market is wide, about 106% or more, and you have a fair price from somewhere else.
  • Make only when you can watch the order. An order you can't cancel in time is a bet at a stale price.
Rule of thumb: on a fee-on-price venue, taking a near 50/50 price costs you about 1.25 to 1.75 points. Posting your own price on Novig or Polymarket US costs nothing. That is the main reason sharp bettors make instead of take.

The taps for each role differ by venue. Each venue page walks through both.

05

What a bet really costs

A bet costs you two things: the spread and the fee. The screen shows neither as a dollar figure.

One bet, both ways

Take the Under 44.5 from the order book above, on Kalshi. The best price to take is 53%. Makers are already waiting on the Under at 52%.

Under 44.5 on Kalshi · taker and maker
TakerTake the Under at 53%. Fee: 0.07 × 0.53 × 0.47 = 1.74 points. All-in 54.74%, about −121. Filled now.
MakerPost the Under at 52% and wait, in line behind the $16,240 already there. Fee: 0.0175 × 0.52 × 0.48 = 0.44 points. All-in 52.44%, about −110. Only if someone takes it.
Difference2.3 points. One point is the spread. 1.3 points is the gap between the taker fee and the maker fee.

The taker pays 2.3 points for a sure fill. The maker keeps them and takes the fill risk from section 04.

The fee, venue by venue

Fees come in two shapes.

  • A cut of your profit (ProphetX). You pay 2% of what you win. A losing bet costs nothing extra.
  • A fee on the price (Novig live, Kalshi, Polymarket US). The formula is a rate × P × (1 − P). That is largest at 50% and shrinks toward the ends. You pay it when the trade fills, win or lose.

Here is the same bet at 55% (−122) on every venue, with the fee folded in. The all-in percent is the number to compare with a sportsbook.

The spread between the cheapest and the most expensive row is almost two points, or about nine cents of American odds. That is often bigger than the edge you are trying to capture. Fees decide which venue you bet at, and sometimes whether to bet at all.

Do this math once per bet, not in your head. Pick the venue, maker or taker, and pregame or live. The calculator gives the all-in price.

The short version

  • Betting pregame and taking a posted price? Novig costs nothing extra.
  • Betting live? ProphetX's 2% of winnings is the cheapest of the four for a taker.
  • Want to post your own price? Novig and Polymarket US charge makers nothing. Kalshi charges makers a small fee on sports games.
  • Want the most money to bet into? Between the two sports exchanges, Novig usually has more. Kalshi and Polymarket US can be deep on main lines. Check market by market.
