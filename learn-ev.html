+EV

Expected value is the only kind of bet a winning bettor places. If a wager is +EV in the long run, it makes money; if it isn't, it doesn't. Picks, hot takes, gut calls — all noise. The math behind +EV, in plain English, plus how to spot it in lines you already see.

What is it?

Once you can strip the vig, you know the true probability behind any line. The next question every winning bettor asks: does the price you're being offered beat that probability?

If it does, the bet is +EV — positive expected value. Long-term, you make money on it. If it doesn't, it's −EV, and long-term, you lose. There's no middle ground.

Expected value is the average dollar amount you'd make per bet if you placed this exact wager an infinite number of times. Most bets you can place at a sportsbook are −EV by design. Finding the few that aren't is the entire job.

The math behind it

If a bet's true probability of winning is 55% and you're getting +100 odds (decimal 2.0), here's what happens on average per $100 risked:

Expected value of a 55% bet at +100 odds Win (55%): +$100 × 0.55 = +$55
Lose (45%): −$100 × 0.45 = −$45
──────────────────────────
EV per $100 risked = +$10

That's a +10% edge. Place that same bet a thousand times and you'd expect to make about $10,000. Of course, you won't ever get the same bet a thousand times — but every +EV bet you find chips away at the books in the same way.

The general formula:

EV percentage EV% = (true probability × decimal odds) − 1

Why it matters more than you think

Most bettors confuse "I won that bet" with "that was a +EV bet." They aren't the same thing. You can lose a +EV bet — and you will, often. You can win a −EV bet and feel like a genius. The result of any single bet tells you almost nothing about whether it was a good bet to make.

EV is signal. Result is noise.

Over thousands of bets, the +EV side always wins. Over twenty bets? Anything can happen. A bettor placing only +EV bets can be down 8 units after a month. A bettor placing only −EV bets can be up 12 units after the same month. After three years, those numbers always invert.

If you stop chasing wins and start chasing +EV, your bankroll's outcomes look completely different over the next 1,000 bets — even if the next 20 still feel rough.

What sharps do about it

They hunt for +EV obsessively, and they pass on everything else.

Most of their work is finding a price somewhere that beats the no-vig fair odds. That means everything starts with devigging (Lesson 01). Once they have the true probability, they compare it to the price they're being offered. If the math says +EV, they bet — even if it's a side they "don't like." If the math says −EV, they pass — even when their gut screams lock.

The discipline isn't picking winners. It's only making bets the math approves of, and trusting that the long run will sort the rest out.
