Arbitrage Betting

Locking in guaranteed profit across two books. The basic math is easy. The part nobody writes about is what to do when you place one side and the other disappears before you can lock it in.

What is an arb?

An arbitrage bet is a pair of wagers on the two sides of the same market, placed at different sportsbooks, where the combined prices guarantee a profit regardless of outcome.

The key condition: the implied probabilities of both sides sum to less than 100%. When that's true, you can size the two stakes so you collect the same payout whichever side wins — and that payout is bigger than what you put in.

A basic 2-way arb
FanDuelEagles +120 (decimal 2.20)45.5% implied
DraftKings49ers −105 (decimal 1.952)51.2% implied
Sum of implied: 96.7% < 100% → arb exists.
Guaranteed profit margin: ~3.3% of total stake.

The price gap shows up because the two books disagree on which side is the favorite, or one of them has a stale line, or one is running a promo. Same outcome, two different prices — you scoop the difference by betting both.

The math: how to size the stakes

For a 2-way arb, you want the payout (stake × decimal odds) to be equal on both sides. Then no matter which side wins, you collect the same amount.

Formula
Stake Atotal × (1/dec_A) ÷ (1/dec_A + 1/dec_B)
Stake Btotal × (1/dec_B) ÷ (1/dec_A + 1/dec_B)
Guaranteed payouttotal ÷ (1/dec_A + 1/dec_B)
Profit margin1 − (1/dec_A + 1/dec_B)
Worked example — $1,000 budget on the +120/−105 arb above
Eagles stake (FD)$470.41at +120
49ers stake (DK)$529.59at −105
Total wagered$1,000.00
If Eagles win$470.41 × 2.20 = $1,034.90
If 49ers win$529.59 × 1.952 = $1,033.76
Guaranteed return: ~$1,034 on a $1,000 outlay. +3.4% locked in, regardless of who wins.

That's the whole concept. Find a market where two books disagree by enough to overcome both vigs, size the stakes by the formula, place both, collect the spread. Repeat.

The arb calculator handles the math for you — but understanding the formula matters because the next section is about what happens when this clean two-step doesn't work.

Where arbs come from

Arbs exist because sportsbooks don't price as a single market — they price independently, then react to each other. Most arbs come from one of four sources:

  • Model disagreement. Each book runs its own pricing model. When two models materially disagree on the same game, prices diverge until enough sharp action corrects the slower book.
  • Promo boosts. One book boosts a market for marketing reasons — Bills moneyline boosted to +180 from +130 — and the boosted price is suddenly out of whack with the rest of the market. The other side of that market at any sharp book becomes an arb leg.
  • News lag. Injury, weather, lineup change hits one book's feed first. The slower book sits on the old price for 5-60 seconds before adjusting. That window is an arb.
  • Soft books and recreational bettors. Smaller books price for their casual customer base, not for the sharp market. Their lines can drift far enough from sharp consensus to create persistent arbs against Pinnacle, Circa, or Bet365.

Arb scanners (OddsJam, RebelBetting, the free tier at Pick the Odds) surface these in real time. The math is automated. The hard part is execution.

The execution problem — and what to do when one side disappears

Here's the scenario every arber faces:

  1. You spot a clean +3% arb. Eagles +120 at FanDuel / 49ers −105 at DraftKings.
  2. You log into FanDuel, type in your Eagles stake, click "place bet."
  3. FanDuel takes 4 seconds to confirm. Bet placed at +120. Good.
  4. You switch to DraftKings. The 49ers line is now −115. Or the market is suspended entirely.

Your arb just turned into half an arb. Now what?

The single most important skill in arbing isn't finding arbs — it's knowing what to do in the 30 seconds after one side disappears.

You have four options. Each has its own math, and the right answer depends on what side A's standalone value looks like.

Option 1 — Take the worse price on side B

Place the second leg anyway at the new (worse) price. The arb becomes a small guaranteed loss. Measure the loss before you click.

Option 1 math — Eagles already at +120, 49ers now −115
Side A locked$470 on Eagles at +120 (decimal 2.20)
Side B new price49ers at −115 (decimal 1.870)
Stake B to balance$553 on 49ers
If Eagles win$470 × 2.20 = $1,034
If 49ers win$553 × 1.870 = $1,034
Total wagered$1,023
Guaranteed payout: $1,034 on $1,023 wagered = +1.1% still locked in.
The price moved against you, but the arb still works. Take it.

The threshold: as long as 1/dec_A + 1/dec_B < 1 with the new price, there's still an arb. The margin shrunk from 3.4% to 1.1%, but it's still guaranteed. Place it.

If the new sum >1 (the arb is dead), you're choosing between guaranteed loss vs. holding side A naked. That's options 2-4.

Option 2 — Hedge at a third book

The 49ers might still be available at another sportsbook you have an account at. Even if the price is worse than your original target, it might be better than DraftKings' new price. Check 2-3 other books before you accept a loss.

Workflow: keep tabs open on Caesars, BetMGM, FanDuel (you used it for side A but can't bet 49ers there either way — pick a different book), Bet365. Whichever has 49ers closest to your original target price is your hedge venue. Run option 1's math on that price.

Option 3 — Hold side A as a naked bet

This is the option most arbers don't realize they have. If you placed side A at a sharp price (you did — that's why the arb existed), then side A is a +EV bet on its own.

Option 3 math — Eagles standalone EV
Your priceEagles +120 (45.5% implied)
No-vig market~50% true (Eagles fair around +100)
Probability gap: +4.5%.
EV%: ~+9.1% on the standalone Eagles bet.
This is a perfectly profitable bet, you just have to live with the variance now.

Decision criteria for holding naked:

  • Is the standalone bet +EV? Run it through Bet X-Ray. If yes, holding is mathematically fine.
  • Can your bankroll absorb the variance? Arb stakes are usually large (you're sizing for a small guaranteed margin). Holding a $500 stake naked is a swing your roll might not be built for.
  • Are you mentally disciplined to not chase? The temptation to "make it back" after a losing naked-hold is what kills most arbers. Hold only if you'd be fine losing the full stake.

Option 4 — Cash out side A

Most US books offer a cash-out option on placed bets. Books haircut the cash-out price by 5-15% versus fair, so this is usually the worst option. Use it only if:

  • The cash-out loss is smaller than the guaranteed loss from forcing the bad hedge in option 1
  • You're not psychologically OK holding the naked position (option 3)
  • You're trying to avoid book attention — a placed-then-cashed bet looks different to risk algorithms than an unhedged arb

Decision tree at a glance

Side B disappeared — pick the right play
SituationBest move
Side B available at slightly worse price, arb still positive Take it. Locked profit shrinks but stays.
Side B available, but new sum > 100% (no arb) Check 2-3 other books for side B. If anywhere still arbs, take it.
No book has side B and side A was a sharp price Hold naked. The standalone bet is +EV.
No book has side B and side A was a soft/recreational price Cash out if available; the underlying isn't +EV on its own.
Bankroll too small to absorb the naked variance Take the worse hedge / cash out even at a loss. Survival > profit on one cycle.
Suspected book stuck flagging an unhedged arb pattern Cash out or take an imperfect hedge. Looking like a normal bettor is worth a few % loss.

Practical workflow to minimize the problem

  • Place the side most likely to move first. If one of the two books is known to move fast (FanDuel, DraftKings), place the OTHER side first. The slow-mover holds while you race to the fast book.
  • Use two devices or two tabs side by side. Have both bet slips ready before you click anything. The win is in the seconds.
  • Pre-fill both stakes before placing either. Stake calculator open, two bet slips populated, then submit both as fast as possible.
  • Bet smaller than you want to. Larger stakes get trade-review at most books — a manual reviewer holds your bet for 30 seconds while their algorithm checks limits. That 30s is enough for the other side to vanish. Stay under the threshold that triggers review.
  • Skip "trade pending" books for arbing. Some books hold bets for trader approval before confirming. Use them for side A (so it gets approved), but never trust the second leg to a slow-confirming book.
  • Pre-compute the "what if" prices. Before you click side A, know what side B would need to be priced at for the arb to break even. That way if the price moves, you can decide in one second instead of ten.
The arber's golden rule: never bet side A unless you're ready to hold it naked if side B disappears. If you'd panic in that scenario, the bet is too big or the underlying isn't sharp enough.

The limit problem — why books hate arbing

Arbing is structurally adversarial. The book makes its margin from vig on each side; an arb extracts profit at the book's expense (one of the two books loses money on every cycle, even if neither knows which one). They've gotten very good at spotting arbers, and they cut you fast.

Common red flags that get you flagged:

  • Stake-calculator outputs. $235.04 screams "arb-calc copy-paste"; $235 or $250 looks like a normal bet. Our arb calc shows you the exact stake AND a rounded-to-nearest-$1 stake side-by-side so you can pick the rounded one without breaking the math. Use it.
  • Speed of acceptance — placing within seconds of a price change suggests automated scanning
  • Multi-book correlation — books share data via third-party services; if your $400 Eagles bet at FanDuel correlates with a $470 49ers bet at DraftKings 90 seconds later, you're a known entity
  • Always taking the line — never the opposite — recreational bettors hit both sides over time; arbers only ever take whichever side is mispriced for them
  • Promo-only behavior — only placing bets that match boosted offers
The rounding habit: if the calc says bet $470.41, just bet $470. The arb margin shrinks by a few cents but you stop telegraphing "I'm running a stake formula." Train yourself to round every stake before you click. Our arb calc does this for you in a dedicated column — no mental math required.
Lifespan of an arbing account: 2 weeks to 6 months at any given book. Soft books cut faster. Plan for it — your bankroll math should assume each book gives you a finite number of cycles before limits drop to $1-50 per bet or new bets get declined entirely.

How to extend account life:

  • Round your stakes to $25 or $50 increments where possible
  • Mix in some "recreational-looking" bets — parlays, popular games, big-name favorites
  • Withdraw less frequently — books flag accounts that deposit, win, and immediately withdraw
  • Use multiple books per arb — spread the pattern recognition across the portfolio
  • Don't only arb. Take some straight +EV bets and some standard bets to round out the profile

Exchanges (ProphetX, Novig) — the no-limit alternative

Everything in the previous section assumes you're betting at traditional sportsbooks where the house takes the other side. There's a different kind of book where the limit problem mostly evaporates: peer-to-peer betting exchanges like ProphetX (use code QEO8QG at signup) and Novig.

On an exchange, you're not betting against the book — you're betting against another user. The platform makes its money on a flat trade fee (typically 1-3% of net winnings), not on you losing. They don't care which side wins or how often you win. There's no gubbing, no flagging, no limit drops on winners.

Why that matters for arbing:

  • You don't get cut. A successful arbing pattern at PrizePicks, FanDuel, or DraftKings will get you limited within weeks. The same pattern at ProphetX or Novig can run indefinitely.
  • You can be the maker. Post your own offer at the price you want. If someone takes it, you got your fill. If nobody does, you walk away. No price-slippage between scanner and slip.
  • Pricing tends to mirror the sharp market. Exchange prices converge to consensus because anyone offering an off-market price gets picked off by other users. They're often the "sharp side" of an arb pair against a softer traditional book.
  • Near-zero vig. Some exchanges charge no commission on losses (only on net winnings); some charge nothing on either side. The implied vig is way under traditional books' 4-10%.
A typical exchange arb
Traditional bookEagles +135 at FanDuel (boost)42.6% implied
Exchange49ers −115 on ProphetX53.5% implied · ~near-fair
Sum of implied: 96.1% → 3.9% arb.
FanDuel will limit you for hitting boosts. ProphetX won't — they make their flat fee either way.

The catch: liquidity

The constraint isn't a book-imposed limit on you — it's how much money is sitting on the other side of the market waiting to be matched. Exchanges are smaller markets:

  • Big-game NFL moneylines on PX often have $5,000-$50,000+ of liquidity available at the posted price. Plenty of room.
  • Smaller markets — NHL props, midweek MLB sides, college games — might have $100-$1,000 sitting on the other side. You can hit them, but a $2,000 stake will eat through the available liquidity and leave the rest unfilled (or filled at worse prices as you walk up the order book).
  • Very thin markets — first-half lines, niche props, smaller leagues — may have no liquidity at all. The price is posted but no one's there to take the other side.

The mental model swap: at a traditional book, your stake size is limited by what the book will let you bet. At an exchange, your stake size is limited by what other users have offered to take. The first cap shrinks as you win. The second cap is fixed by the depth of the market.

Practical play: use exchanges as the sharp-side leg of arbs you find at traditional books. The traditional book gives you the mispriced soft side (with limit risk); the exchange gives you the fair hedge (with liquidity risk). Together, you get a sustainable arbing operation that traditional-book-only arbers can't run for more than a few months at a stretch.

Other trade-offs

  • Smaller menu. Exchanges focus on mainstream US sports (NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, college). Niche markets and most prop ladders are thin or absent.
  • Slower order matching. A market order at FanDuel confirms in < 2 seconds. A market order at ProphetX might take 5-10 seconds to fill, longer if the liquidity is partial. The arb execution window matters more.
  • Regional restrictions. ProphetX and Novig are licensed in fewer states than the big books. Check eligibility before building your workflow around them.
  • Funding frictions. Deposits and withdrawals can be slower than mainline books. Plan your bankroll cycle accordingly.

None of those break the use case — they just shape it. If you can post limit orders, accept that not every bet will fill instantly, and you're betting in markets they cover, exchanges are structurally the best venue for sustained arbing.

Arbing vs. straight +EV — when to use each

Two valid edges, very different shapes
TopicArbing+EV betting
Per-cycle return Small (1-4%) Variable (1-15% per bet)
Variance Near zero (guaranteed) High — wins and losses both real
Capital requirement High — needs balance at multiple books Medium — bankroll on each book individually
Skill needed Speed, execution, scanner usage Pricing intuition, sharp-source identification
Limit speed Fast — books target arbers explicitly Slower — pattern is less obvious
Stress level High during execution windows Lower per bet, higher cumulative variance
Career arc Short on each book, sustainable in aggregate Longer per book if you mix action

The right answer for most bettors is both, in a portfolio. Arbs lock in known returns when the opportunity presents — usually around promo boosts, news lag, or soft books. +EV betting carries variance but extends your account life and scales better with skill. Run both, track each separately, and treat the arbs as a small steady stream while the +EV plays do the heavy lifting on long-run profit.

Arbing is the easiest way to learn execution. +EV betting is the hardest way to learn pricing. Most serious bettors start with arbs and graduate to +EV.

Caveats

  • Bonus bets, free bets, and odds boosts change the arb math. A "$5 bonus bet" is not worth $5; size your hedge against the actual cash value (typically 60-80% of face). Use the bonus converter.
  • Voids and pushes can break arbs. Push rules differ across books. A spread of 7 that pushes at one book and grades as a loss at another (different point) is a partial arb at best. Read the rules.
  • Same-game prop arbs are tempting but often illusory — the "two sides" of an alternate-line market may have different correlations or grading rules across books. Stick to clean 2-way markets (moneylines, spreads, totals) for clean arbs.
  • Withdrawal delays. Books can hold withdrawals on "high-volume" accounts for 5-30 days. Build that into your cycle planning.
  • Tax matters. Each individual win is reportable; net losses are limited in deductibility. Arbing's gross volume is high, even at a 2% margin — the IRS sees the gross.
