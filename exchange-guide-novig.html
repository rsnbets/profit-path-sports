Novig

A sports exchange where pregame takers pay no fee. Reading the screen, taking a price, and posting your own.

Novig operates through Ludlow Exchange, approved June 16, 2026. It dropped its sweepstakes product and relaunched with real money August 4.

This page assumes you have read Exchanges 101: percent odds, the order book, and the difference between taking a price and making one. Here is how those work on Novig's own screens.

Pregame or live

This is the choice that sets your cost on Novig. Pregame takers pay nothing. Live takers pay the fee in section 04. The same tap costs more once the game has started.

Check the money available at your price before you confirm. If your bet is bigger than the top level, not all of it fills at that price.

03

Posting a maker order

Make by posting your own price, and you wait for someone to take it. It may never fill. Makers pay nothing on Novig and earn credits.

05

Quirks that trip people up

  • Novig adds its own liquidity when a market is thin. Some of the money on the screen is the venue, not outside bettors.
  • The fee switches on at game time. A pregame take is free. The same take live is not.
  • There is a lot of money here. About ten times ProphetX's weekly volume in early October, and much more money on the screen in our own pulls.

06

First-trade checklist

  • Check the venue is open in your state before you deposit. Age rules vary from 18 to 21 by venue and state.
  • Convert the price to American odds if you are comparing with a sportsbook.
  • Add the fee. On Novig a pregame take is free and a live take is not. Use the all-in price, not the screen price.
  • Check the money available at your price. If your bet is bigger than the top level, not all of it fills at that price.
  • Read the settlement rules for props and overtime. Contracts settle on their written rules, and some have no push.
  • Cancel posted orders you no longer want. A resting order can fill hours later, after the news has changed.
  • Log the price you got and the closing price. Beating the close is the honest measure of whether you bet well.
