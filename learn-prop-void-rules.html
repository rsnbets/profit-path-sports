Player Prop Void Rules: The Settlement Map

When the player you bet on doesn't play, your locked-in conversion can break — quietly, expensively, while you're not watching. Different books handle void / settle / action differently, and when FanDuel and ProphetX disagree, the math doesn't add up. Here's the cross-book matrix, the sport-by-sport rules, and the protocol to never get caught off-guard.

Why this lesson exists — the silent failure mode

You found a clean bonus-conversion candidate: a player anytime-scorer prop at +500 on FanDuel, hedge at -500 on ProphetX. You placed both legs. Math is locked, ~$8 cash is yours either way. You go about your day.

Two hours before first pitch, your player is a late scratch. He's not in tonight's lineup. Now what happens?

That depends entirely on how each book handles "the player didn't play":

  • FanDuel might void the bet (refund your bonus credit, no win/loss recorded) — OR FanDuel might settle the bet (player didn't hit a HR → "Over 0.5 HRs" loses, bonus is gone).
  • ProphetX might void the bet (refund your $42 stake) — OR ProphetX might settle the bet (no HR → "Under 0.5 HRs" wins → you collect +$8 cash).

When FD and PX agree on how to handle it (both void, or both settle), the math still works out — you either get a clean reset or you get your locked-in $8. When they disagree, your locked-in conversion can break. The worst case: FD settles the bonus as a loss while PX voids your hedge. You burned the bonus credit AND your hedge got refunded — neither side cashed.

This lesson maps the entire decision tree. Read it once, internalize the lineup-check protocol, and you'll never get caught by a scratched player again.

The three settlement outcomes (per book, per bet)

Every book has three possible ways to handle a player prop when the player's status is ambiguous:

VOID
Bet canceled · stake refunded
Bonus bet: credit returned to your bonus pool, reusable on another bet.
Cash bet: stake refunded in full.
ACTION (SETTLE)
Bet resolved based on actual stat
DNP = player recorded 0 of the stat → Over 0.5 loses, Under 0.5 wins.
Used when book counts "did not play" as a real result.

A rarer third state — NO ACTION / PUSH — applies to specific scenarios (rain-suspended games, partial completion). For player props on a DNP it's almost always VOID or ACTION, not push.

The critical question: does FD do the same thing PX does?

The cross-book outcome matrix

Here's what actually happens to your conversion in every possible FD × PX combination, assuming a $10 FD bonus bet at +500 hedged with a $42 PX cash bet at -500 (the standard worked example from the Free Money Guide).

* The "worst case" isn't actually a loss of real money — your bonus credit (which wasn't your cash to begin with) is consumed and your hedge stake is refunded. You're net-neutral on real dollars but you "used up" a bonus bet you could have converted instead. If you have spare bonus credits in your FD pool, you just try again with a different player. If this was your last bonus credit and you really wanted to convert it, that's a missed-opportunity loss.

The math, summarized: in 4 of 5 scenarios you either come out ahead or break even. The 1-in-5 "worst case" only costs you a re-do, not real cash. Bonus conversion via player props is robust to the void-rule variance — as long as you follow the lineup-check protocol below, it's a manageable risk.

Sport-by-sport rule quirks

Every sport has its own player-prop edge cases. Memorize the ones that affect the bets you're actually placing.

⚾ MLB
  • DNP / scratched: most books VOID. Some settle if the player took 1+ plate appearances (i.e., didn't start but pinch-hit).
  • Pinch-hit appearance: usually counts as "action" — bet stands and settles on actual result.
  • Rain-suspended games: bet typically voids if game doesn't reach official status (5 innings, or 4½ if home team leading).
  • Pitcher changes: pitching-related props (K's, ER, etc.) often void if the listed starter doesn't start. Doesn't apply to hitter HR props.
🏀 NBA
  • Minimum minutes rule: most books require 1+ minute played for the prop to have action. If player checks in for 30 seconds, bet stands.
  • DNP / out (injury, rest): bet VOIDS at most books.
  • Late scratch (within 90 min of tip): VOID at FD / DK / MGM. PX usually follows the same.
  • OT minutes count toward totals by default — relevant for points / rebounds / assists props.
🏈 NFL
  • Inactive on the gameday list: VOIDS at all major books. Inactives post ~90 min before kickoff.
  • Active but no snaps: typically settles as ACTION (Over loses on no stat). Rare scenario.
  • OT counts for anytime TD scorer / yardage props by default.
  • Lateral / fumble recovery TDs: usually count for anytime TD scorer unless explicitly excluded.
🏒 NHL
  • Scratched (not dressed): VOIDS at all major books and PX.
  • Dressed but logs 0 shifts: rare; typically settles as ACTION.
  • Empty-net goals: COUNT for anytime goal scorer.
  • Shootout goals: DO NOT count for anytime goal scorer (only regulation + OT goals count).
  • OT counts for regulation/OT-inclusive props; check market wording.

The general rule: when a player is FULLY scratched (not in any lineup, not on the bench), most books VOID. When the player is on the roster and sees ANY action (1 PA, 1 minute, 1 shift), most books settle as ACTION.

Book rules change. Always read your specific book's house rules for player props before relying on them — search "[Book name] house rules player props" or check the FAQ on the book's site. Settlement rules tend to be in fine print but they're public.

The lineup-check protocol

The single most important habit to prevent void-rule problems: confirm the player is in tonight's lineup before placing either bet. The rules below stack — follow them all.

  1. Wait for lineups to post. Each sport posts at different times:
    • MLB: starting lineups typically post 2-3 hours before first pitch (sometimes earlier for day games).
    • NBA: game-day injury reports post ~5 hours before tip; final lineups closer to tip (~90 min out).
    • NFL: inactive list posts 90 minutes before kickoff. Plan around this.
    • NHL: morning skate lineups give the day's intentions; final lineups closer to puck drop.
  2. Verify on a reliable lineup source. Use the team's official Twitter/X account, MLB.com / NBA.com / NFL.com / NHL.com lineup pages, or aggregator sites (RotoWire, Lineups.com).
  3. Double-check the lineup spot. For MLB: is the player in the starting lineup AND batting in the order? For NBA / NFL / NHL: confirm "active" / "in" / "dressed" status.
  4. Same player, same stat line, same threshold on both books. If FanDuel says "Player X · Anytime Home Run," ProphetX must show "Player X · Over 0.5 Home Runs." The player name spelling, the stat type, and the threshold all have to match exactly.
  5. Have a backup candidate ready. Late scratches happen — especially in MLB (rest days, weather, last-minute injuries). Bookmark 2-3 alternate plays so you can pivot in seconds if your first choice gets pulled.

This protocol takes ~3-5 extra minutes per conversion. It eliminates the worst-case scenario almost entirely. Don't skip it.

Edge cases worth knowing

A few less-common scenarios that can still trip you up:

  • Weather suspension (MLB). If a game is suspended after both bets are placed but before becoming "official" (5 innings completed, or 4½ if home team leading), both books typically VOID. Both legs refund cleanly.
  • Postponement (MLB / NFL). Both books VOID if game is postponed and rescheduled. Caution: if the game is just delayed to later that day, bets stand. If rescheduled to a new date, void.
  • Game shortened by injury / forfeit. Edge case — depends on book. Usually voids; verify with the book.
  • Player traded mid-season. If a player is on Team A when you place the bet but traded to Team B before the game in question, the bet typically voids. Rare in-season but happens at trade deadlines.
  • NBA "load management" announcement post-bet. If a star is announced "out for rest" between when you placed and tip-off, prop VOIDS. Cash-out wisely if you have any concern — but bonus conversions don't allow cash-out on the bonus side anyway.
  • NHL goalie shootouts. Shootout goals don't count for anytime goal scorer (regulation/OT only). Empty-netters DO count.
  • Pitcher changes (MLB). Hitter HR props don't void when the pitcher changes — only pitching props (strikeouts, ERA) void on a listed-pitcher change. Hitter props are about the hitter's action, not the pitcher's.

What to do when it happens

Even with the lineup-check protocol, a late scratch will eventually happen to you. Here's the recovery flow:

  1. Check both bet slips immediately. Open FanDuel and ProphetX. Look at the relevant bet. The status should show as "Open," "Settled," or "Void."
  2. Wait for the game to settle. Even if your player didn't play, the game still happens. Both books need the game to finish before they can grade your bets.
  3. Check the grading. After the game ends:
    • Both VOIDED → stake/bonus refunded on both sides. You're net-neutral. Re-deploy.
    • Mixed (one voided, one settled) → check the outcome matrix above. Usually you're net-neutral to slightly positive.
    • Both SETTLED (rare on a DNP) → standard outcome. Your conversion held.
  4. If something looks wrong, contact support. Each book has a clear chat/email support channel. Reference the specific bet slip ID and explain what happened. Most prop-grading errors are resolved within 24 hours.
  5. Log the outcome in your tracker. Tag it as a "void-affected conversion" so you can see how often it happens and refine your protocol over time.

The bottom line: on a properly-set-up bonus conversion with the lineup protocol followed, void rules might cost you a re-do attempt — they almost never cost you real money. The risk is operational, not catastrophic. Stay disciplined, stay patient, and the conversions will compound.

The bottom line

Player prop void rules sound scary, but they're mostly a paper tiger for bonus conversion. The math is structured so that even when FD and PX disagree, you usually end up neutral or ahead — almost never down real money. The only consistent "loss" is the missed-opportunity cost when your bonus credit gets consumed on a DNP and the hedge gets refunded (you used a bonus credit without converting it).

Three habits prevent this almost entirely:

  1. Wait for lineups before placing either bet. Patience beats speed.
  2. Match the exact player and stat line across both books. Mismatched market wording = mismatched settlement.
  3. Keep backup candidates ready so you can pivot in 30 seconds if your first choice gets pulled.

Do those three things, and the void-rule risk shrinks to a footnote. The conversions compound, the bonus pool empties out, the cash hits your account. The math is your friend; the lineups are your timing tool.
