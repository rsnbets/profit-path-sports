What is this reading, in plain English?

You see a row on Pick the Odds with prices and dollar amounts across Pinnacle, Circa, ProphetX, and Novig. Type those numbers into the cards above — the tool runs the full analysis: translation rule on the exchanges, devig on the books, consensus fair, and the target price to beat at a soft book. Market quoted somewhere else too? + Add more sources — another sharp book, or prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket — and they join every calculation.

The amounts mean different things: Pinnacle and Circa are book limits (the max they'll book at that line); ProphetX and Novig are exchange depth (real user-posted money). Leave any source blank if it isn't quoted.

The five outputs, decoded
  • Sharp Translation — flips exchange offers to the sharp's actual position and tallies sharp capital per side, with the imbalance ratio. The risk-adjusted line recounts it by capital actually at risk (depth numbers are win-amounts — laying favorites costs more per dollar of depth). If venues point opposite ways the read is flagged and the verdict softens; opposite-side entries at prices summing under 100% get an arb-compatible warning — that flow may be an arber's two legs, not conviction.
  • Book Stance — two dialects, one question: which side is the book afraid of? The lower-limit side is the side it's protecting (Circa's language); when limits are balanced — Pinnacle's usual posture — the read falls back to price lean: whose probability the book prices above consensus. Books + exchanges agreeing = aligned signal; disagreeing = down-weight it.
  • Conviction Check — when exchange depth exceeds book limits, sharps wanted more capacity than the books would underwrite. Strong conviction; a leading indicator of line movement. The headline compares total exchange money to total book capacity for the market-level version of the same read.
  • Consensus Fair — weighted no-vig fair across the sources (Pinnacle and Novig weighted heaviest).
  • Soft-Book Target — the price to beat at a soft book to capture cross-market EV. Primary side = where the sharp money leans.
Reading the verdict — and the caveats

Strong (5×+ imbalance) · Solid (2–5×) · Mild (1.3–2×) · Flat (under 1.3× — no signal, just take the consensus fair). "Books confirm" bumps the verdict up a level; "mixed book stance" demotes it. "Books-only lean" = no exchange depth but asymmetric limits — treat as a lean, not a conviction.

  • Book limits are confidence indicators, not sharp positions — the tally uses only exchange depth.
  • On thin markets ($100–$300 depth) a single rec bettor can mimic a sharp signal. Bigger depth, better read.
