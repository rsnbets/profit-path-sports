★ Key Point — A Thin Edge Needs a Sharp Number
The EV math here is exact — but the fair odds you typed in are an estimate, not a fact. Small edges are real money over hundreds of bets if that fair price is trustworthy — and pure noise if it isn't. So the thinner the edge, the sharper your fair price has to be: devig a sharp market (Pinnacle, Circa, a consensus line), never a soft book. If a couple-cent error in your fair odds would erase the edge, treat it as fragile. New here? The lesson +EV walks through it in plain English.
How to use this

What it does. Compares the price a sportsbook is offering you against the fair, no-vig price — and tells you whether the bet is worth making. Output: expected value in % and dollars, the break-even hit rate, and a Kelly-sized stake.

How to use it.

  • Enter your odds — the price you can actually bet at any book.
  • Enter the fair / no-vig odds — the true price after the vig is stripped out. Don't have it? Run the market through the Devig Calculator first.
  • Enter your stake to see EV in dollars.
  • Read EV %: positive (green) means the bet is +EV and worth placing; negative (red) means skip it.

The numbers.

  • True Win % — the real chance of winning, from the fair odds.
  • Break-Even % — the win rate your price needs just to break even. If True Win % beats Break-Even %, the bet is +EV.
  • EV % and EV $ — your edge, per dollar and on your actual stake.
  • Kelly Sizing — how much to bet given your bankroll. Quarter-Kelly is the default; most pros bet quarter or half, never full.

Garbage in, garbage out. EV math is only as good as the fair odds you feed it. A fair price devigged off a soft book (BetMGM, Bet365) isn't sharp — devig the sharpest market available (Pinnacle, Circa, or a sharp consensus).
