## Thesis in one paragraph

The US sports betting industry, fully unleashed by the 2018 Supreme Court overturn of PASPA, has restructured American sports — converting fans into transactional gamblers, exposing athletes to gambler harassment and death threats, and quietly adopting a "ban or bankrupt" operational model (the **European model**, in industry terms): limit the few customers who could possibly win, push high-margin parlays at the unsophisticated masses. The leagues, who spent the 20th century arguing in Congress that sports betting was *evil* (Tagliabue, Stern, Vincent under oath in PASPA hearings 1991-92), have done a complete 180 to take their cut of the revenue. Funt's title is the thesis: when you tally the social cost — athlete harassment, families destroyed by addiction, fan relationships to sports corroded, integrity scandals returning — **everybody loses** except the books and the operators of the political machinery that legalized them.

## Key frameworks / named concepts

### 1. The "Ban or Bankrupt" Model (CRITICAL)
> *"The 'European model' (also known as 'ban or bankrupt') is seen as a way of making up for that knowledge gap: throw out sharps and push high-margin parlays on the unsophisticated masses."*
>
> — Robert Walker, Nevada bookmaker (formerly MGM Mirage), ch 5

**Funt's explicit naming of the equilibrium PROFITPATH is formalizing.** Walker frames it as a European import — European bookies had superior tech but poor American-sports knowledge, so they limited their way to profitability. American operators copied the model rather than investing in skilled bookmaking. Direct quote justifies our paper's framework. **Major source.**

### 2. "Gaming" vs "Gambling" — The Linguistic Strategy
> *"Frank used to say 'gaming' has very few negative connotations compared to 'gambling.' You don't have people who go to jail because of 'gaming' or who commit suicide or leave their wives or have family problems."*
>
> — Jeffrey Derevensky, gambling-psychology expert, recalling AGA founder Frank Fahrenkopf

The American Gaming Association was founded in 1994 specifically to launder "gambling" into the more anodyne "gaming." Industry employees are coached to use the latter. Funt explicitly refuses: "I'll be calling it what it is: gambling." **Direct corruption-of-language insight** — relevant for our anti-book framing.

### 3. The Sportsbook Profiling Sequence (Funt's documentary detail)
From PointsBet trader interviews (ch 5):

**From account opening:**
- $1,000 "risk-free" first-bet promotion acts as a "honey trap"
- Customer's bet size on this promo categorizes them:
  - **<$1,000:** low-value square (target customer)
  - **>$1,000:** potential high-value (could be sharp)
  - **=$1,000:** "mid-wit" — knows enough to be profitable without true sharp skills
  - True sharps know not to bet exactly the offer
- Profile decided in first 3 bets — whether they win or lose is largely irrelevant; *betting patterns* are the signal

**The signals that get you limited:**
1. **Beating the closing line consistently** (THE primary signal — same as LOSB)
2. **Chasing steam** — quickly betting outdated lines after market-mover moves
3. **Shopping for best odds across books** (yes, just basic comparison shopping)
4. **Betting unusually large on long-odds favorites** (the "honey trap" outcome)

**The limit ladder:** max-bet trim → segment restriction → "limited to a penny" → account closure.

### 4. "Limited to a Penny" — The Regulatory Loophole
NJ prohibits sportsbooks from banning rule-abiding customers for winning. So sportsbooks limit them to **$0.01 bets** — technically allowing them to bet. NJ's Division of Gaming Enforcement spokesperson, asked about this loophole: *"We are not in a position to evaluate hypotheticals."* (Direct quote, ch 5.) Documents the practical irrelevance of the apparent legal protection.

### 5. The Sportsbook Economic Model (insider view)
> *"These companies are built on losers."* — Former DraftKings employee (ch 5)

> *"This is an entertainment activity. People who are doing this for profit are not the players we want."* — Jason Robins, DraftKings CEO (ch 5)

**Trader economics:** $50k starting salary; sometimes only 2 traders covering 15 NBA games. **Books don't INVEST in skilled bookmaking; they limit.** The economic logic: paying for trader expertise is more expensive than just kicking out anyone who shows signs of beating mispriced lines.

**Trader insider trading:** PointsBet traders themselves bet at OTHER books, using inside info. "There's no rules against insider trading." Former FanDuel employee: *"Tons of traders"* there bet at other companies; they got limited because they were winning.

### 6. The Beard Economy
Pros use "beards" — friends, family, paid accounts — to circumvent limits. Names a real industry within the industry:

- **Account access deals:** sharp uses beard's account; either 50/50 split or 85/15 (sharp gets 85% of wins, beard owes nothing on losses — a "free roll")
- **"Fill it" orders from syndicates:** sharp says "Texans -2.5 for $15,000" and a syndicate (often using bot armies) fills the order across many accounts. Sharp pays nothing up front. Syndicate gets the *information* (often "tails" the sharp's bet). If picks lose, sharp owes the money.
- **Disguises:** sharps in jersey of opposing team, fake mustaches, cowboy hats (when rodeo's in town). One man wore a full gray beard + cane + frumpy sweater to look elderly.
- **Whale flipping:** Funt's source "Alex" recruits losing rich gamblers in hot tubs in Hawaii, then "flips" them into sharp partners. Books are slow to re-classify someone with a long losing history.

### 7. The Funt Beard Experiment (empirical demonstration)
Funt himself, working with sharp Isaac Rose-Berman, ran a 2024 experiment:
- Rose-Berman sent $3,000 via PayPal, Funt bet through his own FanDuel account
- "Priming": placed several large MLB parlays first to look like a "borderline addict who FanDuel thinks might end up dumping thousands"
- Then placed a complex tennis bet using Rose-Berman's modeled edge
- Down ~$5k after a week, FanDuel limited tennis to **$62.50 per bet** (was $4,000 before) but kept baseball at $27,000
- Funt's interpretation: *"This is the perfect refutation of the sportsbooks' claim that they 'don't ban winners.' That's true, they ban people they think/know are likely to win."*
- After a week of inactivity, FanDuel offered a 50% deposit-match promo — appetite for losing bets was limitless. **Direct empirical evidence** that profiling is real and fast.

### 8. The Industry Hypocrisy Triangle
Funt's reporting catches the books' own dissonance:
- FanDuel billboard: *"TURNS OUT, WATCHING FOOTBALL CAN REALLY PAY OFF"* (last two words underlined for emphasis)
- DraftKings ad on train: *"ALL ABOARD THE MONEYLINE"*; *"OUR FAVORITE SHADE OF BLUE? GREEN"*
- ROGA (Responsible Online Gaming Association, founded 2024 by FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, et al.): ads should NOT suggest you can profit.
- When Funt reads the FanDuel ad to ROGA director Jennifer Shatley, she responds: *"I can't speak to what people will think based on that."*

**The PR layer's incoherence is documented in their own words.** Useful for the paper.

### 9. PASPA Hearings (1991-92) — The Leagues' Original Position
For our paper's historical/regulatory section, Funt documents the league commissioners' explicit pre-2018 stance under oath:

**Paul Tagliabue (NFL):** *"Sports lotteries inevitably foster a climate of suspicion about controversial plays and intensifying cynicism with respect to player performances, coaching decisions, officiating calls, and game results. Cynical or disappointed fans would come to assume 'the fix was in'... Once a state legalizes sports gambling, it will be extremely difficult for other states to resist the lure."*

Also said: *"Sports gambling is evil at any age."*

**David Stern (NBA):** *"What sports betting does is transform the betting line into the bottom line. When our fans begin to leave games feeling disappointed or cheated even though 'their' team has won, that spells trouble."* Per a longtime colleague: *"David thought gambling in general was corrupting and corrosive. He hated the idea of state governments telling people, 'Hey, bet the grocery money on gambling.'"*

**Fay Vincent (MLB):** *"Americans' identification with their heroes and teams is unique. It would be tragic if the importance of batting averages and baseball cards were overshadowed by betting odds and lottery tickets."*

**Bill Bradley (NJ Senator, ex-Knicks):** *"I don't want to see athletes turned into roulette chips."*

These are the same leagues that, post-2018, signed sweeping partnership deals with sportsbooks for billions of dollars in fees and integrity-data payments. **The 180-degree reversal is the central indictment** Funt builds the book around.

### 10. The "Cheering in the Wrong Places" Pattern
David Stern's phrase. Cited by Bill Bradley from a personal experience: Knicks up 8 in the dying seconds; bench-warmer scores to bring it to 10; Madison Square Garden fans **booed** — they'd bet the Knicks against the spread, and the late basket cost them.

**The transformation of the fan-team relationship** — from "rooting for the team to win" to "rooting for the result to match my bet" — is Funt's central social critique. Cited again via Bob Costas: *"It's one more thing that makes sports more transactional than emotional."*

### 11. Old Vegas vs. New American Sportsbooks (counter-example)
Funt's contrasting case study: **South Point Hotel, Casino & Spa** (Las Vegas). Director Chris Andrews + colleagues Jimmy Vaccaro and Vinny Magliulo, combined 147 years experience.

- Welcome sharp action; **use it to refine odds** (LOSB's market-maker model in practice)
- "Old Las Vegas" — bookmakers comp meals for known winners like pro bettor Bill Krackomberger (*"I beat them every Super Bowl, and they still want to take care of me."*)
- Took record $185.6M in bets on 2024 Super Bowl
- Counter-example to "ban or bankrupt" model **proves it's a choice, not a structural necessity**

### 12. The Carson Barrett Opening Gambit
Walk-on Purdue role player; meaningless 3-pointer with 35 seconds left covers a 27-point spread; gets death threats: *"I hope you fucking die. Kill yourself for taking that 3 you fucking worthless loser."* NCAA's Clint Hangebrauck: harassment is *"a wildfire that's just burning out of control"* since 2018. The author's opening proof-of-concept that legalization has real human cost.

## Weak claims / dated material / criticisms

- **Funt is not a quantitative/analytics source.** Doesn't model markets, doesn't critique pricing in technical depth. Strengths and weaknesses both lie in the journalism.
- **Some sources are pseudonymous** (Tony, Alex, "former PointsBet trader") — required for the access, but reduces academic citability. Mitigated by Funt naming his sources where possible (Eccles, Walker, Krackomberger, Walls, Rose-Berman).
- **Anti-industry framing is consistent and explicit.** Funt's POV is that legalization was a bad call. Worth flagging that the book is advocacy-journalism, not neutral reporting. Doesn't invalidate the findings; affects citation framing for the paper.
- **Most data is anecdotal, not aggregated.** Funt documents many individual cases of athlete harassment, limited accounts, addicted gamblers — but doesn't (in chapters read so far) provide population-level prevalence data. Need academic/regulator sources for that.
- **Some recency claims will date fast.** 2024 ROGA founding, 2024 Super Bowl numbers, etc. Topical but ephemeral.

## Pedagogical patterns

Funt is journalism. Different toolkit than Miller's analytical mode. Key moves:

### Effective patterns
- **Open on a human story** (Carson Barrett death threats), then zoom out to the structural cause. The pattern repeats — every chapter opens with a person.
- **Direct quotes from insiders** with explicit attribution where possible. The DraftKings CEO admission, the FanDuel cofounder reversal, the trader interviews — each carries weight because Funt got the source to say it on the record.
- **The author embedded in the story.** Funt admits he bets occasionally, ran the beard experiment himself, sat in on the pro bettor breakfast. **Credibility-by-participation** vs the typical academic-remove. Disarms the "you don't get it" objection.
- **Documentary juxtaposition.** 1956 Larsen "NO BETTING" sign / 2024 FanDuel ad. FanDuel billboard / ROGA director's words. Pre-PASPA Tagliabue / post-2018 league deals. **The contrast is the argument.**
- **Catalog the bad** then refuse to flinch. The death-threat messages reproduced verbatim including profanity. The 50% deposit-match arriving after a $5k loss. Reader feels the weight.
- **Honest about complications.** Funt admits his own ambivalent relationship with betting. Acknowledges the "everyone agrees to be interviewed only if not named" pattern. Tells readers when sources declined.
- **Historical sweep with specific characters.** PASPA hearings aren't an abstraction; they're Tagliabue, Stern, Vincent, Bradley, Bryant — named, quoted, anchored. Same with the Black Sox, Pete Rose, Sherman White, Jack Molinas. The history is *people*, which makes it stick.

### What we can borrow for PPS
1. **Lead with a person, not a concept.** When teaching the Ban-or-Bankrupt frame, open with Beau Wagner (or our own anonymized example) before introducing the model.
2. **Use the documentary-juxtaposition move.** PROFITPATH already does this slightly ("Sportsbooks expect you to lose. Disappoint them.") but Funt's level — pairing two specific things from two specific dates — is sharper.
3. **Catalog the gritty specifics rather than abstract.** "Limited to $0.01" is more memorable than "severely limited." "FanDuel offered a 50% deposit match after we lost $5,000" is more memorable than "books send promos to losing accounts."
4. **Embed the writer's perspective transparently.** Where appropriate, PPS content can acknowledge "I bet too, here's what I've seen" — builds credibility without compromising the analysis.
5. **Use real names and brand names.** Funt names DraftKings, FanDuel, PointsBet, BetMGM, Robins, Eccles, Walker explicitly. Anti-paywall, anti-vague.

### What to avoid
- Funt's tone gets close to **doom journalism** at times. PPS is pro-bettor, not anti-betting. We borrow the rigor and specificity without inheriting the elegy.

4. **The trader-economics argument for limiting.** No public source has clearly explained: *books limit because trader salaries + workload make skilled bookmaking expensive, and limiting is cheaper than getting better.* Funt's PointsBet interviews surface this; nothing public says it plainly. **Major gap.** *(Funt)*
5. **"Gaming" vs "gambling" as a deliberate corporate linguistic strategy** — known by the industry, never publicly explained. *(Funt)*
6. **The "honey trap" sign-up bonus mechanics.** Almost every "sportsbook review" site promotes signup bonuses as customer benefits. Nobody explains they're profiling-instrument-first, customer-benefit-second. Big gap. *(Funt)*
7. **The "limited to a penny" regulatory loophole.** State gaming regulators (NJ DGE quoted in Funt) decline to address it. Massive gap between law-as-written and law-as-experienced. *(Funt)*
8. **The fan-experience transformation argument.** Bill Bradley, David Stern, Bob Costas all named it (rooting for spread vs. team, "cheering in the wrong places"). Popular sports betting media never discusses it. *(Funt)*
