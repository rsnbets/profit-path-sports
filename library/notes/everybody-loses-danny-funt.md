## Thesis in one paragraph

The US sports betting industry, fully unleashed by the 2018 Supreme Court overturn of PASPA, has restructured American sports — converting fans into transactional gamblers, exposing athletes to gambler harassment and death threats, fueling a gambling-addiction crisis (21% of NJ sports bettors have wished they were dead), and quietly adopting a "ban or bankrupt" operational model: limit the few customers who could possibly win, push high-margin parlays at the unsophisticated masses. The leagues that testified under oath in 1991-92 that sports betting was *"evil at any age"* (Tagliabue) have done a complete 180. Two dozen sportsbooks have shuttered or sold in 7 years; FanDuel + DraftKings control ~75% of the market. State governments accepted ridiculously low tax rates (6.75% Iowa, 10% in most) in the name of "competition," only New York set a sustainable rate (51%). The countries that legalized first (UK) provide a cautionary tale the US chose to ignore. Funt's title is the thesis: when you tally the social cost — athlete harassment, families destroyed by addiction, fan relationships corroded, integrity scandals, bankruptcies, suicides — **everybody loses** except the books, the leagues, and the political machinery that legalized them.

## Key frameworks / named concepts

### 1. The "Ban or Bankrupt" Model — Explicit Industry Naming (CRITICAL)
> *"The 'European model' (also known as 'ban or bankrupt') is seen as a way of making up for that knowledge gap: throw out sharps and push high-margin parlays on the unsophisticated masses."* — Robert Walker, Nevada bookmaker (formerly MGM Mirage), Ch 5

**The exact framework PROFITPATH is formalizing — named within the industry.** European bookies had superior tech but poor American-sports knowledge, so they limited their way to profitability. American operators copied the model rather than investing in skilled bookmaking. Direct quote justifies our paper's framework. **Primary source.**

### 2. "Gaming" vs "Gambling" — Deliberate Linguistic Strategy
> *"Frank used to say 'gaming' has very few negative connotations compared to 'gambling.' You don't have people who go to jail because of 'gaming' or who commit suicide or leave their wives or have family problems."* — Jeffrey Derevensky on AGA founder Frank Fahrenkopf, intro

AGA founded 1994 specifically to launder "gambling" into "gaming." Industry employees coached to use the latter. Funt refuses: *"I'll be calling it what it is: gambling."*

### 3. The Sportsbook Profiling Sequence (PointsBet trader interviews, Ch 5)

**From account opening:**
- The $1,000 "risk-free" first-bet promotion acts as a "honey trap"
- Bet size on this promo categorizes you:
  - **<$1,000** = low-value square (target customer)
  - **>$1,000** = potential high-value (could be sharp)
  - **=$1,000** = "mid-wit" — knows enough to be profitable without true sharp skills
  - True sharps know not to bet exactly the offer
- Profile decided in first 3 bets — whether win/lose largely irrelevant; *patterns* are the signal

**Surefire ways to get limited:**
1. **Consistently beating the closing line** (primary signal)
2. **Chasing steam** — quickly betting outdated lines
3. **Shopping for best odds across books**
4. **Betting unusually large on long-odds favorites** (the honey trap outcome)

**The limit ladder:** max-bet trim → segment restriction → "limited to a penny" → account closure.

### 4. "Limited to a Penny" — The Regulatory Loophole (Ch 5)
NJ prohibits banning customers for winning, so books **limit them to $0.01 bets** instead. Funt has seen customers limited to $3.63 (Beau Wagner) and $62.50 (Funt's own beard experiment). NJ Division of Gaming Enforcement spokesperson on the loophole: *"We are not in a position to evaluate hypotheticals."*

### 5. The Sportsbook Economic Model — Insider Acknowledgments (Ch 5)
> *"These companies are built on losers."* — Former DraftKings employee

> *"This is an entertainment activity. People who are doing this for profit are not the players we want."* — Jason Robins, DraftKings CEO

**Trader economics:** $50k starting salary; sometimes only 2 traders covering 15 NBA games. **Books don't invest in skilled bookmaking; they limit.** Cheaper to kick out potential winners than to pay smart bookmakers.

### 6. Sports Betting Innovations — Historical Timeline (Ch 1)
The four foundational innovations that built modern bookmaking:
1. **Vigorish (1907)** — NYC dice maker John H. Winn. From "vinegar" (bitterness of betting at an inherent disadvantage).
2. **Point spread (1930s)** — NYC math teacher Charles K. McNeil (his pupils included John F. Kennedy). Spotted underdogs points to make matchups more relatable than odds-only formats.
3. **Props (1986)** — Caesars Palace sportsbook manager Art Manteris invented for the 1986 Super Bowl. The "Refrigerator" Perry anytime-TD prop. **Modern prop betting is 40 years old.**
4. **Bookmaking software (mid-1980s)** — Vic Salerno's Leroy's company became the first sports-betting listing on a US stock exchange.

### 7. PASPA Hearings (1991-92) — The Leagues' Original Stance (Ch 1)
- **Paul Tagliabue (NFL):** *"Sports gambling is evil at any age."*
- **David Stern (NBA):** *"What sports betting does is transform the betting line into the bottom line."*
- **Fay Vincent (MLB):** *"It would be tragic if the importance of batting averages and baseball cards were overshadowed by betting odds and lottery tickets."*
- **Bill Bradley (NJ Senator, ex-Knicks):** *"I don't want to see athletes turned into roulette chips."*
- Senate vote: 95-5
- Carved out Nevada, Oregon, Montana, Delaware

**The same leagues post-2018 signed partnership deals with sportsbooks for billions.** The 180-degree reversal is Funt's central indictment.

### 8. The Fantasy Sports Loophole (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**UIGEA 2006** (Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act) banned financial transfers to illegal gambling but included a **fantasy sports exemption** championed by MLB. This loophole:
- Was exploited by Chris Fargis (2007 blog post: "What if fantasy seasons were shorter?")
- Spawned the DFS industry
- Created FanDuel (Nigel & Lesley Eccles, founded from Scotland)
- Was the gray-market beachhead from which the eventual sports-betting legalization push was waged

The legalization wasn't "popular demand" — it was a calculated industry strategy from DFS operators who had grown enough customer base + capital to lobby for the bigger market.

### 9. The Lobbying Playbook (Ch 3)
**Jeremy Kudon** (Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe) ran the post-Murphy state-by-state legalization campaign. Key mechanics:
- FanDuel and DraftKings stayed in the background; **NBA + MLB were the public face**
- Same firm represented both leagues AND sportsbooks; *"almost 100% overlap"* in lobbyists
- Wining-and-dining lawmakers at games (Illinois Bulls 2019, etc.)
- Famous athletes brought to statehouses
- Result: state lawmakers signed off on *"everything FanDuel and DraftKings wanted"*

### 10. The "Build the Plane While Flying" Defense (Ch 12)
Industry & regulators claim post-2018 legalization was unprecedented and chaotic by necessity. **Funt's counter:** the US deliberately ignored 20 years of UK experience. UK had legalized online gambling in 2005; clear lessons existed. Joshua Grubbs (U New Mexico psychologist) on US lawmakers: *"I have not spoken to a single state legislature that was involved in legalizing sports betting that was reading the research out of the U.K."* The chaos was a *choice*, not a fact of life.

### 11. The VIP Economy (Ch 6)
- **VIPs = 2-3% of bettors, 60-70% of revenue.** Pareto on steroids.
- FanDuel + DraftKings each employ **several hundred "hosts"** to recruit and cater to VIPs
- Perks: free first-pitch ceremonies (announced as "sportsbook representatives" because the PA system *"wouldn't tell the world you're a loser"*), helicopter rides to PGA Championships, pickup basketball on NBA courts, NFL sideline access, bachelor-party cruises
- Hosts trained as friendly bros to encourage continued losses — the modern equivalent of casino comps for whales
- Matt the school teacher (Ch 10) was a VIP — DraftKings shipped his freebies to his school to avoid his wife seeing the packaging

### 12. The Hold-Inflation Plan (Ch 11)
**FanDuel CEO Amy Howe announced (Oct 2024):** wants to push average hold from **12% → 16% by 2030**.
- Mainly via parlay push (parlays are the highest-margin product)
- Coloradans were told books would hold ~5% when voting on legalization
- 16% norm would mean **$600M/yr more lost in Colorado alone** than what legalization promised
- Wall Street analyst warning: *"At some point you break your customers by beating them too fast, and the next thing you know, the experience is not fun. It's not entertainment if you're beating people to a pulp."*

### 13. The State Tax Reality (Ch 11)
- **Most states tax sports betting at 6.75-10% of gross revenue** — extremely low.
- Iowa + Nevada tied at lowest: 6.75%
- Industry veteran quote: *"Every other state should start with 20 percent."*
- **New York's accidental 51% rate** generated **$2 billion in 3 years**
- Other states scrambling to copy: Ohio 10→20%, Illinois moved to graduated 20-40%, MD/MA proposing increases
- Sportsbooks' standard objection (taxes are too high) doesn't survive contact with the math — most states are leaving billions on the table

### 14. The Bookmaker Bloodbath (Ch 11)
**Counterintuitive finding:** legalization didn't enrich most sportsbooks — most went broke.
- Half-trillion wagered in 7 years
- 24+ operators shuttered or sold off by 2025
- WynnBET, FOX Bet, SI Sportsbook, Barstool Sportsbook, Tipico, Betfred, PointsBet all gone or scaled back
- DraftKings cumulative EBITDA 2018-2023: **−$4.3 billion**
- FanDuel didn't post a profitable quarter until 2022
- The duopoly that survived (FD + DK) won via head start from DFS days (15M+ existing customers, deep-pocketed marketing)

### 15. The Adaptation Hypothesis (Ch 10)
Scholarly hypothesis: most people learn to moderate gambling when exposure is stable; addiction rates settle around 1%. **Las Vegas evidence supports it** — longtime residents have addiction rates near national average; new residents have among the highest. **But:** adaptation requires *stable* opportunities. Sportsbook apps are *constantly* updating with new bet types, shorter intervals, new dark patterns — *no time to adapt.* The current generation of US sports fans is bearing the brunt of the learning curve.

### 16. The Federal Excise Tax & Market-Maker Discrimination (Ch 11)
Derek Stevens (Circa owner) gripe: the **0.25% excise on handle** favors retail books over market-maker style books.
- Example: Circa handles $100M @ 3% hold = $3M revenue → pays $250k tax
- Hard Rock handles $50M @ 6% hold = $3M revenue → pays $125k tax
- *Same revenue, half the tax for the higher-hold book.*
- Stevens calls it *"the illegal bookmaking preservation tax"* — it pushes business offshore

### 17. The Touts/Picks Economy (Ch 7) — The Anti-PPS
Richart Ruddie's Sports Information Traders. The mechanics:
- Sells gambling picks at $16k packages
- AI-generated content; touts subscribe to competitors' services and resell their picks
- Hedge with affiliate commissions: gets 35% of customer's *net losses* if customers sign up via Ruddie's affiliate links
- *"It's like a stockbroker giving picks, but with sports"* — but with no transparency, no accountability, no performance track record
- **The industry PPS positions against directly.** Funt's reporting documents how this entire industry preys on the same population PPS wants to teach.

### 18. The In-Play Heckler Phenomenon (Ch 8)
Bryson DeChambeau at 2024 US Open at Pinehurst — first US Open played in a sports-betting state. Spectators in the gallery now openly heckle players based on their props.
> *"Hey, Bryson! I've got a hundred bucks on you to shoot over 70.5 today!"* (Lee Woody, drinking White Claws)

Carson Barrett territory but in real-time, in person, mid-tournament. Funt's PGA Tour source: *"Golf presents the ultimate prop betting opportunity."* 45% of golf money bet at DraftKings in 2024 was during rounds, not pre-tournament.

### 19. The Integrity Erosion Pattern (Ch 9)
- **Luka Dončić (2023):** down 2 in final seconds vs Warriors, missed layup, glared at ref while rubbing thumb-and-fingertips (the universal "money" gesture). Fined $35k.
- **Rudy Gobert (2023):** same gesture two weeks earlier. Fined $100k for doubling down: *"I know the betting and all that is becoming bigger and bigger, but it shouldn't feel that way. I think it's hurting our game."*
- **Adam Silver's "good for integrity"** promise (2015) vs the reality of stars publicly accusing refs of gambling-related corruption.
- **Leigh Steinberg quote:** *"Integrity is not some abstract concept... I've called gambling an existential threat. The amount of incidents that have occurred just in a short time frame ought to send a chill up the spine of everybody in a league office."*

### 20. The Responsible Gaming Theater (Ch 10)
The most damning systemic finding in the book:
- PointsBet RG department = **one person** (Teresa Fiore) for 300k+ customers in 14 states. Later got 1 analyst.
- *"Your job is to maximize value to the shareholders. It's not to be a good, moral citizen."* — Former PointsBet exec
- **Sportsbooks embrace the lowest common denominator** for consumer protections; anything beyond that is "competitive disadvantage"
- "Bet responsibly" — Mark Gottlieb (Northeastern Public Health): *"frankly, nauseating."* Marlene Warner: *"It's finger wagging. People who have gambling problems don't want to have them."*
- **"Intermittent reinforcement schedules"** — psychology term for sportsbook UX engineering "to stimulate the most intense dopamine experience possible"

### 21. The NJ 2023 Rutgers Stats (Ch 10) — The Population-Level Cost
- NJ gambling-disorder rate: **3× national average** (national ~2%)
- 18-24 age group at high risk: **19%**
- Sportsbook ads make 41% more likely to bet
- **21% of sports bettors have wished they were dead**
- **10% have attempted suicide** (vs <1% for non-sports gamblers)
- Gambling disorder has **50%+ lifetime suicidal ideation rate** — highest of any addiction
- Brian Hatch (CT counselor): *"In every recovery room, there's always somebody who's also addicted to drugs or alcohol. And without fail, all of them say that gambling was the hardest one to stop."*

### 22. The UK Cautionary Tale (Ch 12)
- UK legalized online gambling 2005 — 13 years before US
- London has **more gambling parlors than Starbucks + McDonald's locations combined**
- 2024 University of Glasgow study: **8× more UK adults struggle with gambling problems than previously thought**
- 10%+ of young UK men who gambled in past year had considered suicide
- UK now proposing **affordability checks** — books would review bank statements before high-volume customers can keep betting
- **The US ignored every lesson** of the UK experience

## Examples / case studies worth preserving

### Carson Barrett (Intro) — Death threats over a meaningless 3
Purdue walk-on hits a 3 with 35 sec left in a 75-50 win, covering a 27-pt spread. Receives messages like *"I hope you fucking die. Kill yourself for taking that 3."* NCAA's Hangebrauck: harassment is *"a wildfire that's just burning out of control"* since 2018.

### Beau Wagner (Ch 5) — Marketing vs reality in 24 hours
- 42yo Chicago attorney; bet $1,000 on Evan Fournier to be top scorer in Knicks-Celtics at 50:1
- Fournier scored 41 — Wagner won $50,000
- DraftKings retweeted his ticket: *"BEAU KNOWS BETTING"*
- Next morning: limited to $100 on NBA winners, $3.63 on player props

### Matt the elementary school teacher (Ch 10) — VIP grooming as predation
- Connecticut, lifelong New England fan, started during pandemic to pass time
- $10/$20 bets → $5,000/day long-shot parlays
- $100k+ blown in 18 months, including money intended for house down payment
- DraftKings shipped him VIP freebies *to his school* (because he couldn't have DraftKings packaging delivered home where his wife would see)
- **DraftKings never asked what he did for a living or whether he could afford to gamble.** He's an elementary school teacher.
- Last bet October 10, 2022. Now in weekly GA + counseling.

### Rob Minnick (Ch 10) — Georgetown dream destroyed
- New Jersey kid, Georgetown dream school since 8th grade
- Pandemic + DFS app on dorm laptop + a classmate to sit with → cascade
- Stopped going to spots outside his room. *"Saw the library for the first time senior year."*
- Hit bottom at 5am in a Rome hotel-room shower with phone, losing cost of trip on roulette
- Last bet November 12, 2022 — credit-card binge over 12 hours
- Now works for problem-gambling nonprofits

### The Funt Beard Experiment (Ch 5) — Empirical proof of pre-emptive profiling
- Funt + Isaac Rose-Berman, 2024
- $3,000 PayPal stake → priming with MLB parlays → real tennis bet using modeled edge
- Down ~$5k after a week → FanDuel limited tennis to $62.50, kept baseball at $27k
- *Then* FanDuel sent a 50% deposit-match promo — appetite for losing baseball bets was limitless

### Matt Davidow + son (Ch 11) — The vig lesson
- LOSB co-author teaching his 8-year-old son the math of gambling
- Coin flips with skewed payouts (Dad gets $1.10 for wins, son gets $1)
- Son immediately realizes: *"with the vig working in your favor, you get all the coins eventually"*
- **Direct cross-reference between Funt and our other primary source.** Davidow as a character in Funt's narrative gives us a literary thread between the analytical and journalistic perspectives.

### Bill Wadlow Self-Exclusion Trap (Ch 10)
- NJ poker player; self-excluded at BetMGM to control urges
- A month earlier had placed a 4-leg parlay paying $55,250 if it hits
- 3 of 4 legs won (Schauffele PGA, Celtics NBA, Alcaraz Wimbledon) — needed Yankees in MLB
- Self-exclusion **automatically refunded** his $500 stake despite FanDuel's site saying *"Active and pending wagers will not be cancelled"*
- The screenshot he relied on turned out to refer to FanDuel-direct exclusion only — state-list exclusion (which auto-applied) had different rules
- Suing FanDuel

### Mr. 300,000 (Ch 5) — The "square whale" model
Super Bowl 2024: customer wants to bet $300k cash on 49ers at South Point. Director Andrews checks history (lost $22k there previous Super Bowl) → takes the bet. The legitimate "old Vegas" model: accept big bets from known losers, price-adjust on sharp action.

### Amit Patel (Ch 10) — The corporate corollary
Jacksonville Jaguars mid-level finance manager. Stole **$22 million** from the Jaguars through company credit cards to fund DFS/FanDuel/DraftKings. *"Legendarily bad"* DFS player per a peer. Diagnosed post-firing with gambling disorder + alcohol + cannabis + Adderall abuse.

### The Pittsburgh self-exclusion mug shot (Ch 10)
Some states require people who self-exclude to be **fingerprinted and photographed like a criminal**. Pittsburgh casino had a sign in its lobby warning anyone on PA's self-exclusion list who entered would be arrested. *"OK, sign this legal document and we'll help you, and if you violate it by doing the very thing you've just admitted you have a problem with, you'll get thrown in jail."* — Marlene Warner (MA Council on Gaming).
