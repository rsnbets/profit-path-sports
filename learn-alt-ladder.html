Shopping the Alt Ladder

The main line gets sharp money first. The Over 1.5 / 2.5 / 3.5 rungs above it get model-priced and forgotten. Where the soft money lives — and why BetMGM is where this matters most.

What is the alt ladder?

Most props start with one "main" line — the one the book leads with. For a hitter prop, that's usually Over/Under 0.5 hits. For a passing prop, it might be Over/Under 224.5 yards. The book sets that line so the over/under split sits roughly 50/50 after vig.

But the same prop almost always has a ladder of alternates stacked above and below it:

Aaron Judge — hits in tonight's game Over 0.5 hits → −280 (main line)
Over 1.5 hits → +135
Over 2.5 hits → +480
Over 3.5 hits → +1500
Under 0.5 hits → +220

Same player. Same game. Same underlying question (how many hits will Judge get?). Five different bets, five different prices. And not all five are priced consistently with each other.

If the main line is right, every rung above it has a "fair" price. When a rung disagrees with the main line, one of them is wrong — and the rungs are wrong far more often than the main.

Why alt rungs get mispriced

The main line is the most-watched market on a prop. Sharp money hits it first, books move it fastest, the price tightens up quickly. By kickoff or first pitch, the −0.5 line is usually pretty efficient.

The rungs above it are different. They're:

  • Lower volume. Way fewer bets land on Over 2.5 than Over 0.5, so books don't get the same correction signal from the market.
  • Auto-generated from a model. Books typically price alt rungs off an internal distribution, not from market action. If the model is off, every rung is off — sometimes in the bettor's favor.
  • Updated less aggressively. When the main line moves on news (lineup change, weather, injury), the alt rungs often lag.
  • Tier-priced by traders, not algorithms, on some books. A human picks "+135 looks right for Over 1.5" without grinding the math. Sometimes they're right. Sometimes they're 30¢ off.

Result: an alt rung often disagrees with its own book's main line by 5-15% in implied probability. That's free EV if you spot it.

How to spot the soft rung

Three-step workflow. Doesn't need a model — just an odds calculator and a sharp benchmark for the main line.

Step 1. Find the sharp price on the main line. Pinnacle, Circa, or a no-vig consensus across 5+ books works. That's your benchmark "true" probability for the prop.

Step 2. Translate that into what the alt rungs should price at. For a hitter prop, you can do this from a Poisson or empirical distribution — but a quick rule of thumb works: if the main line says 70% to get a hit, rungs further from the main move predictably (Over 1.5 maps to roughly 35-40%, Over 2.5 to ~15%, depending on at-bats).

Step 3. Compare. Pull up the ladder at BetMGM, FanDuel, DraftKings, Caesars — whatever you have. If one book's Over 1.5 is priced at +180 while consensus says fair is +130, that's a +EV bet, even if their main line at −280 is in line with everyone else.

Worked example — hits prop, fair main line at −250 (71.4% true) Pinnacle main: Over 0.5 at −250 → fair price
DraftKings: Over 0.5 at −260 → fair, no edge
FanDuel: Over 1.5 at +145 → fair (~−5¢ vig)
BetMGM: Over 1.5 at +185 → +EV ~6%

You don't need every book on every prop. You need one book to disagree with the consensus on one rung. That's the entire game.

BetMGM specifically

BetMGM is the book where alt-ladder shopping pays off most often. A few reasons:

  • Their alt-rung pricing is heavily model-driven and lightly traded. You'll see rungs that haven't moved since they were posted, even after lineup news shifted the main line by 20¢.
  • They lean into "boost" promotions on rungs. Boosted alt prices on baseball, basketball, and football prop ladders show up daily. The boost is calculated off their already-soft rung, which can stack into significant edge.
  • Their same-game-parlay legs use rung pricing. If the underlying rung is mispriced, that mispricing flows through to SGPs they let you build. (Doesn't fix the SGP markup problem covered in Parlays & SGPs, but it's a softer starting point than other books.)
  • Their player-prop menus are deep. More rungs per prop than most competitors → more chances for one of them to be off.
Practical habit: after you've decided you like a prop based on the main line, always sweep the BetMGM ladder before you bet. Roughly 1 in 4 times, a rung is priced better than the main for the side you wanted — and the only cost is 30 seconds of clicking.

This isn't a knock on BetMGM as a book — they're fine to use, they pay out, the app works. It's just the reality of how their pricing is structured. Their main lines are sharp, their ladder rungs are soft. That gap is your edge.

Where else this shows up

  • Caesars — rungs often lag main-line moves by several minutes around news. Less consistent than BetMGM but worth a check.
  • FanDuel — generally tight on rungs, but their player-prop boosts on the home page sometimes price below fair.
  • DraftKings — main line and ladder tend to be priced consistently. Less inefficiency, but more reliable for sizing.
  • Hard Rock, Fanatics, Bet365, ESPN BET — newer/smaller books often have very stale ladders, especially on lower-volume sports (NHL, MLS, college baseball). Worth a sweep when you have an opinion.

The main line is the storefront. The alt ladder is the back room. The back room is where the money is.

Caveats & risks

  • Soft rungs get you flagged faster than soft mains. Books know which markets they're weak in. If you only ever bet BetMGM Over 1.5 rungs and never their main lines, you'll see limits drop quickly. Mix your action.
  • Variance is brutal on long-odds rungs. An Over 2.5 hits bet at +480 is +EV at +500 fair — but you're going to lose this bet 80% of the time. Size accordingly. Use Kelly on the implied edge, not a flat-unit habit.
  • Rungs are correlated to the main. If your main-line read is wrong, every rung above it is also wrong. Garbage in, garbage out.
  • Some "boosts" are designed to look juicy and aren't. Always plug the boosted price into a calc and compare to fair, not to the un-boosted version. "+250 boosted from +185" can still be −EV if fair is +320.

And don't miss the easier version of this idea — books often list the same exact bet twice on the same site. See Same Bet, Two Prices for the equivalent-market version of the play.
