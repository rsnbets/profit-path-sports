Warning
Books to avoid
Some books have track records that aren't worth the risk — affiliate dollars or not. We don't link these because we don't recommend them.
⚠ Avoid
Bovada

Offshore book operating out of Curaçao — outside U.S. legal protections. If they refuse to pay, there's no realistic path to recovery.

Personal experience

I deposited, claimed a deposit bonus, and got about halfway through the rollover when Bovada limited my account. Bets started showing "under review" — they'd pend for a minute, then the odds would change. Eventually they disabled the account entirely. After five months of emails and support tickets, they re-opened it — but by then the rollover window had passed, so the funds were effectively locked. Never got that money out.

This isn't a one-off. Search matched-betting forums and you'll see the same pattern repeatedly: deposit bonus claimed → halfway through rollover → account limited → funds inaccessible.

If you use them anyway

Deposit small, withdraw fast, and never claim a rollover-attached bonus. Don't keep meaningful balances on the platform.

⚠ Avoid
Borgata

NJ-licensed book with weak geolocation enforcement on signup and a customer-service response to security concerns that should make you walk away. Don't give them personal info.

Personal experience

I'm in Colorado. Borgata operates only in New Jersey. I signed up on their site and made a deposit — no problem. The platform happily took my card and identity. When I tried to place a bet, the geolocation check finally caught up and blocked me. Fine, no big deal — I withdrew the money cleanly.

Shortly after, my card information was compromised. I contacted Borgata to close the account and flag what had happened. They closed it — but didn't seem the least bit concerned that my data had been exposed from their platform.

Books make mistakes; geolocation lags, payment processors get breached, things happen. How a book handles a customer reporting compromised data is the signal. The right response is alarm, an investigation, escalation. Borgata's response was a shrug.

The take

Don't give Borgata any personal information. If you're outside NJ and the signup flow lets you create an account, that's not a feature — it's a control failure, and the data you hand over goes to a system that just demonstrated it doesn't take customer security seriously.
