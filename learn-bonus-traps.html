Path 02 · Bonus Hunter

Pitfalls to Watch For

A clean welcome-offer conversion should land 70-80% of face value as withdrawable cash. Most new users land between 0 and 40%. The gap is a handful of specific mistakes — bad qualifier picks, wrong hedge stake, ignored rollover rules. Here's all of them, with the math each one breaks.

Pushes — the trap that kills your bonus

A push is a tie — when a spread or total lands exactly on the line. Mavs −7.5 can't push (the half-point makes it impossible). Mavs −7 can push if they win by exactly 7. Total of 215.5 can't push. Total of 215 can.

Why pushes destroy bonuses: when a bonus bet pushes, most books treat it as a void — they consume the bonus stake and return $0. You'd literally have been better off if the bet lost outright; at least the bonus would be "used."

Lines that CAN push (avoid for bonus bets):

  • Spreads on whole numbers: −7, +3, −10
  • Totals on whole numbers: 215, 47
  • Player props on whole numbers: over 25 points

Lines that CAN'T push (use these):

  • Spreads on .5 or any non-integer: −7.5, +3.5, −10.5
  • Totals on .5: 215.5, 47.5
  • Moneylines (someone always wins)
  • Player props on .5: over 24.5 points

If a sportsbook only offers a −7 spread on the game you want, find a different game. The cost of a push (entire bonus value) is way bigger than any line value you'd get from that specific market. The same goes for qualifying bets in many bet & get offers — push the qualifier and the bonus might never release.

Why account restrictions happen (in plain English)

🛒 THE GROCERY STORE COUPON CAP

You discover a grocery store loophole: stacking manufacturer coupons on top of clearance prices on top of a "spend $50 save $10" deal. You walk out with $80 of groceries for $12 every Tuesday. The math is real, the receipts prove it.

Week 1: cashier doesn't notice.
Week 2: cashier notices, doesn't care.
Week 3: a store manager watches you check out.
Week 4: a new sign goes up: "Max 2 coupons per transaction."
Week 5: your loyalty account gets flagged. You can still shop — but the system blocks the deals.

That's account limits at sportsbooks. When you consistently win — using +EV strategies, arbs, or bonus conversion — books track your account. They don't ban you. They just limit how much you can bet, or which markets they'll accept your action on. You can still place bets. You just can't place the bets that were making you money.

How to delay limits as long as possible: mix your bets (some -EV "fun" bets among the +EV ones), bet sizes that look casual ($23.47 not $50.00), don't withdraw immediately after every win, don't open and close the same account repeatedly. Books look for patterns — patterns that look human take longer to flag.

The honest truth: if you consistently win, you'll eventually be limited at every recreational book. That's why exchanges (ProphetX, Novig) and sharp books (Pinnacle, Circa) exist — they don't limit winners. The full sharp playbook is built around graduating to those venues once the recreational books restrict you.

Rollover — read it before depositing

Rollover (also called "playthrough" or "wagering requirement") is the multiple of the bonus you must wager before withdrawal is allowed. It's the single most important number in the offer terms.

1× rollover — the U.S. norm for sportsbook bonus bets. Bet the bonus once. If it wins, the profit is withdrawable. Conversion ratio ~70-75%. Worth claiming.
3× rollover — must wager 3× the bonus amount. Variance grows fast. Conversion drops to ~40-50%. Marginal — do the math first.
5×+ rollover — usually not worth claiming. The expected loss to vig over multiple wagers eats most of the bonus value before you can withdraw. Skip unless the bonus is huge.

Where rollover applies (and why it matters):

  • Bonus only (most U.S. books) — wager the bonus amount, profit is withdrawable. Best case.
  • Bonus + deposit — must wager (bonus + deposit) × rollover before any withdrawal. Worse — your own money is locked too.
  • Min odds during rollover — some books require all wagers at −200 or longer. Limits your +EV options.

Don't confuse with casino bonuses. Casino bonuses often have 20-50× rollover and are heavily −EV. Sportsbook bonus bets are different. Read the terms to know which kind you're claiming.

Rollover is also their probation period

This is the part that catches most new bonus hunters by surprise. Rollover isn't just a hurdle — it's a gauge. Every bet you place while working through it is data the book uses to decide whether you're a recreational customer worth keeping or a sharp / promo-hunter worth limiting.

Patterns that get you flagged mid-rollover:
  • Always betting the sharpest available line (line-shopping behavior)
  • Bonus bets only on long-odds plays where conversion is highest
  • Stakes near the max allowed for every bet
  • Never placing recreational losers — no parlays, no homer props, no future bets
  • Hedging visibly — same game, opposite side, similar timing

If a book decides you're a sharp before rollover completes, they can limit your account, void pending bets, or in the worst case freeze your funds entirely until the rollover window expires — at which point the bonus is gone and any cash mixed in with it is effectively locked. (This is exactly what happened in the Bovada warning on the homepage.)

The defense: blend in. Sprinkle "normal-looking" bets through your rollover. Mix in same-game parlays, recreational futures, low-stakes losers. Vary your bet sizes. Don't always pick the sharpest available line. The goal is to look like a fan working through a bonus, not a sharp grinding +EV. Once the rollover is complete and the cash withdraws, you can go back to whatever style you actually want to play.

Most U.S.-licensed books are reasonable about this — they're more aggressive at limiting visible winning bettors (the industry calls them 'sharps') but they pay legitimate winnings. Offshore books are where the real risk lives: they can simply refuse to release funds and you have no legal recourse.

Compressing rollover with arbs and low-hold bets. When the rollover requires a lot of wagering and you don't want to bleed value to vig, arbing or low-hold bets are the right tool. The idea: bet both sides of a market across two books so the variance approaches zero. You grind through rollover wagering fast and walk away with minimal cash leakage.

One important nuance: full arbs (where the implied probabilities sum under 100%) are the cleanest math but the most detectable pattern — books spot identical timing on opposite sides quickly. Low-hold bets (sum just over 100%) are safer: you absorb a tiny loss but the pattern reads as ordinary line shopping rather than literal arbitrage. Tools like OddsJam find both in real time.

Done right, low-hold bets get you through rollover with minimal vig and minimal flagging. Done sloppily — same dollar amounts, same minute, perfectly opposite sides — they trigger exactly the limiting we just covered.

Stake and market restrictions

Read the offer terms before you deposit. Common restrictions that change the math or break the play entirely:

  • Min/max qualifier stake — e.g., must bet $5 minimum, or no more than $1,000
  • Min odds on qualifier — e.g., bet must be at −200 or longer to qualify
  • Sport / market exclusions — some bonuses can't be used on certain sports, futures, or live betting
  • One bet per game / one per market — limits how many times you can stack
  • Bonus bet stake limit — max $50 per bonus bet, etc. Limits hedging on long-odds plays
  • Same-game parlays — may or may not count toward rollover; differs by book

The fine print isn't there to be helpful. Read it.

Account and timing pitfalls

  • KYC delays. Books require ID verification before they release withdrawals. Submit your ID early — don't wait until you've claimed a bonus to discover you can't withdraw the conversion.
  • Time limits. Bonus bets typically expire in 7-14 days. Don't claim 5 books at once if you can only realistically convert 2 in that window.
  • One per household. Some books restrict promos to one account per IP, address, or payment method. Roommates can't both claim the same offer.
  • State eligibility. Welcome offers vary state to state. The Colorado offer might not match the Pennsylvania one.
  • Account limiting. Books detect promo abuse. Bonus hunting alone is usually fine; combining it with consistent +EV betting will get you limited faster.
  • Co-mingled balances. Some books mix bonus + cash balances on the deposit screen, making "withdrawable" murky. Track real cash separately from bonus credits.

The general rule

If an offer looks too good to be true, the catch is in the rollover or the restrictions.

Read every word of the terms before depositing. The single biggest mistake new bonus hunters make is rushing into a $1,000 deposit-match offer without checking the rollover. A 5× rollover on a $1,000 match means wagering $5,000 before withdrawal — and most of that bonus value gets eaten by variance and vig before you ever see real cash.

If you can't articulate exactly how the offer pays out and what you have to wager to unlock it, you're not ready to deposit yet.
