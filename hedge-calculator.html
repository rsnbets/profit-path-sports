What a hedge actually is

Back before the season you put $100 on a team to win the title. They made it — the final is tomorrow. Win, and you collect $1,000. Lose, and you get nothing.

You don't have to just sit there and hope. You can place a second bet now — this time on the other team — so you walk away with money no matter who wins.

That second bet is the hedge. Here's the trade:

  • No hedge — your team wins, you get the full $1,000. They lose, you get $0.
  • Hedge — you give up part of that $1,000, but in exchange you collect a guaranteed payout either way. Never the biggest win, never the empty-handed loss.

That's all a hedge is: a bet on the other side that turns "win big or win nothing" into "win something for sure." This calculator tells you exactly how much to put on that second bet.

★ Key Point — Don't Hedge Just Because You're Nervous
A hedge always costs you a little — that's the price of making it a sure thing. So only do it when it's actually worth it:
HedgeThe bet got too big. A futures ticket or DFS slip that's grown into more money than you can afford to lose. Lock in a guaranteed result and stop sweating it.
Let it rideYou still think you'll win. You're just nervous. Hedging here only hands back money you'd probably have kept.
Simple rule: hedge to protect money you can't afford to lose — not to calm your nerves. More on this →
How to use this

What it does. A hedge opportunity usually shows up in one of three spots: a futures ticket close to cashing (your preseason long shot made the final), a live, in-play bet where the odds have swung your way mid-game, or a DFS pick’em slip down to its last leg — where you can bet that final player's prop the other way at a sportsbook. In any of those you've got a bet working, and a hedge is a bet on the opposite outcome that locks in a result instead of leaving it to chance. This calculator sizes that hedge.

How to use it.

  • Enter your original bet — the stake and odds you already have working.
  • Enter the hedge odds — the price on the opposite outcome at any book right now.
  • Pick a strategy, then read the hedge stake and the net result for both outcomes side by side.

The three strategies.

  • Equal Profit — bet the hedge so you net the exact same amount whichever side wins. The most predictable lock.
  • Win Back Stake — size the hedge to recover your original stake. If your original still wins you keep a free-roll profit; if it loses you break even.
  • Custom Amount — set your own hedge size, for partial hedges, live betting, or keeping some skin in the game.

The honest part. Hedging isn't free — see the Key Point above. It converts an uncertain outcome into a certain (usually smaller) one. Use it to lock in over-exposed positions; think twice before hedging away a genuine edge.
