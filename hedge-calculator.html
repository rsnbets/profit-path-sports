What is a hedge, in plain English?

You've already placed a bet that could win big — a futures ticket one game from cashing, a live bet that swung your way, or a DFS slip down to its last leg. A hedge is a second bet on the other side, sized so you walk away with money no matter who wins.

The trade: no hedge means the full payout or nothing. Hedge means you give up part of the max win in exchange for a guaranteed result — never the biggest win, never the empty-handed loss. This calculator sizes that second bet to the cent.

Should you hedge — or let it ride?

A hedge always costs a little — that's the price of certainty. So only pay it when it's worth it:

  • Hedge when the ticket has grown into more money than you can afford to lose. Lock in a guaranteed result and stop sweating it.
  • Let it ride when you're just nervous but the position is still good. Hedging there only hands back money you'd probably have kept.

Simple rule: hedge to protect money you can't afford to lose — not to calm your nerves. More on thinking in positions →

The three strategies — which one do I pick?
  • Equal Profit — the same net result whichever side wins. The most predictable lock; start here.
  • Win Back Stake — the hedge recovers your original stake. Original wins: you keep a free-roll profit. Original loses: you break even.
  • Custom Amount — you set the hedge size. For partial hedges, live spots, or keeping some skin in the game.
