Types of Welcome Offers

Sportsbook bonuses come in four shapes — risk-free bets, deposit matches, bonus-bet credits, and odds boosts. Each one converts differently, and the math is not interchangeable. Treat them all the same and you leave 20-40% of the bonus on the table every time.

Bet & Get

Format: "Bet $5, get $200 in bonus bets" — you place a small qualifying bet of $X, and a fixed amount of $Y in bonus bets lands in your account regardless of whether the qualifier wins or loses.

The play: Bet your $X qualifier on the safest market available — a heavy favorite moneyline, say. The qualifier is roughly break-even because the implied probability of winning is so high. You're not gambling for the bonus; you're paying a small entry fee. Then convert each bonus bet by hedging on a second sportsbook.

Typical bet & get math $5 qualifier risked → ~$5 expected loss
$200 in bonus bets received
$200 × ~73% conversion ratio
──────────────────────────
Net: ~$141 cash for ~10 minutes of work

This is by far the most common welcome offer in the U.S. market. DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, Hard Rock, ESPN BET — most run some variation of bet & get.

Bet & Get — Win Required

Format: "Bet $5 — if your bet wins, get $200 in bonus bets." Same flow as Bet & Get, but the bonus only releases if your qualifying bet wins. Lose the qualifier, no bonus.

The play flips: with the standard Bet & Get, you want the safest qualifier so you don't lose money on it. With the win-required version, you want the qualifier most likely to win — a heavy favorite. The bonus is the prize; releasing it is what matters. Cash upside on the qualifier is secondary.

Win-required offer at $5 qualifier — two strategies Heavy favorite (−300, ~75% win rate)
Win: $1.67 cash + $200 bonus → ~$148 converted = ~$149
Lose: −$5
EV: 0.75 × $149 + 0.25 × (−$5) = ~$111

Coin flip (−110, ~52% win rate)
Win: $4.55 + ~$146 = ~$150
Lose: −$5
EV: 0.52 × $150 + 0.48 × (−$5) = ~$76

Heavy favorite wins by ~$35 EV per offer. Bet a −300 or longer moneyline (a single heavy fav, not a parlay) and accept the small cash payout — the bonus release is what you're paying for.

Read the terms carefully. Some books count a partial cashout or push as "did not win." Others have stricter rules around what qualifies. This format is harder to abuse, which is why books have started shifting to it — they expect bonus hunters to bet long-shots and lose the qualifier ~52% of the time.

Risk-Free First Bet

Format: "First bet up to $1,000 risk-free." Place a real-money bet up to $X. If it wins, you keep the cash. If it loses, you get $X back as bonus bets.

Counterintuitive part: long-odds qualifiers are more valuable here, not less. The downside is the same regardless of odds — you get refunded as bonus bets. The upside scales with the odds.

$1,000 risk-free first bet — two scenarios Bet at +400, wins: → $5,000 cash
Bet at +400, loses: → $1,000 in bonus bets → ~$730 cash
──────────────────────────
Bet at −300, wins: → $1,333 cash
Bet at −300, loses: → $1,000 in bonus bets → ~$730 cash

The longer the odds, the bigger the cash payday if it wins — but the loss outcome is identical. EV-maximizing odds are typically +200 to +400 depending on the book; plug different odds into a bonus calculator and find the sweet spot.

Read the terms: some "risk-free" offers are stake-returned (you get bonus bets equal to your stake), some are stake-not-returned (you lose the cash AND get bonus bets — like a real loss plus a refund). The math differs.

Deposit Match

Format: "100% deposit match up to $1,000 in bonus bets." You deposit $X and the book matches it with $X in bonus bets, often immediately.

The play: deposit the maximum match amount, then convert the bonus bets the same way as bet & get — long-odds bonus bet on Book A, hedge on Book B.

Watch for tiered unlocks: some deposit matches release in chunks — you have to wager $X across the book before each tier of the bonus releases. Calculate the actual conversion across the full unlock cycle before deciding if it's worth claiming. Tiered unlocks at high rollover usually aren't worth it.

Odds Boosts (related, not a conversion play)

Format: "+135 boost on Mahomes 250+ passing yards" when the actual line is +110. The book pays out at the boosted price.

Important: this is not bonus bet currency. It's a real cash payout at inflated odds. Different beast from welcome offer conversion.

The play: Use the EV Calculator to verify the boost actually beats the no-vig fair price. Many "boosts" are still −EV — just less so. Real +EV boosts are good plays at sensible Kelly stakes, but they're not bonus conversion plays. Don't confuse the two.
