The Vig

What sportsbooks really charge you on every line.

What is it?

Every line you've ever bet had a tax built in.

It's called the vig (short for vigorish) — also known as the "juice" or "the hold." It's how sportsbooks make money. Not by predicting games better than you. By pricing every line so the odds add up to more than 100%.

Here's the simplest possible example: a coin flip. Each side has a true 50% chance of winning. What would fair odds look like?

Fair Odds
+100 / +100
→
Book Odds
−110 / −110

Bet $100, win $100 either way — that's fair. What does the sportsbook actually post? −110 / −110. You bet $110 to win $100, on either side. That extra $10 you risk on each bet? That's the vig.

The math behind it

From Lesson 02, you already know that −110 implies a 52.38% chance of winning. Two sides at −110 sum to 104.76% — the math we couldn't explain at the end of last lesson.

Implied probability of both sides at −110 52.38% + 52.38% = 104.76%

That "extra" 4.76% is the vig. It's why two evenly matched teams can both look like a slightly-worse-than-50/50 bet at the same time. The book isn't claiming both teams will win — it's just charging you 4.76% on every coin flip.

Why it matters more than you think

If you've been winning roughly half your bets at −110 and feeling like you should be break-even, here's why you're not:

You need to win 52.4% of your bets at −110 just to break even. Win exactly 50% and you lose money over time — even though it feels like you're picking right half the time.

Cost of the vig at 50% win rate, 1,000 bets at −110 500 wins × +1.00u = +500u
500 losses × −1.10u = −550u
────────────────────────
Net result = −50u

That's 50 units down over a thousand bets — at $100/unit, you've quietly handed the books $5,000. You picked half right. You still lost. That's the vig, draining your bankroll while you can't see it happening.

What sharps do about it

They strip the vig out of every line. They turn −110 / −110 back into 50% / 50% — the true probability — and only bet when the price they're being offered beats the true probability, not the priced one.

Stripping the vig out of a market is called devigging. It's the first move every winning bettor learns to make. Every other concept on this site — EV, Kelly, CLV — depends on it.
