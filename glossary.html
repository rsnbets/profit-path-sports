Betting Glossary

Every betting term you'll run into, written in plain English. No jargon left undefined — and where a term has a full lesson, it's one click away. Bookmark it. When a word stops you, this is where you come.

ABCDE FHIJK LMOPS UV

A

Action
Money you have riding on a live bet. "I've got action on that game" just means a bet is in play.
American odds
The +/− odds format US sportsbooks use. A minus number marks the favorite (bet that much to win $100); a plus number marks the underdog (bet $100 to win that much).
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Arbitrage (arb)
Betting every side of a market — usually at different sportsbooks — at prices that lock in a profit no matter which side wins.
Arbitrage deep dive →

B

Bankroll
The pool of money you've set aside specifically for betting — kept separate from money you actually need.
Bonus bet
A free-bet credit a sportsbook hands you, usually for signing up. If it wins you keep only the profit, not the credit itself — so it's worth less than its face value until you convert it.
Path 02 · Bonus Conversion →

C

Chalk
Slang for the favorite. "Betting chalk" means betting the favorites.
Closing line
The final price on a bet right before the event starts — the sharpest, most-informed number the market produces all day.
Lesson 06 · CLV →
CLV (closing line value)
Whether the price you got beat the closing line. Consistently positive CLV is the best short-run proof that you're a winning bettor.
Lesson 06 · CLV →
Cover
When a team beats the point spread — a favorite winning by more than the spread, or an underdog losing by less than it (or winning outright).

D

Decimal odds
An odds format showing your total return per $1 staked. 2.00 means a $1 bet pays back $2.00. Common outside the US.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Devig
Stripping the vig out of a market to uncover the true, fair probability hiding behind a price.
Lesson 03 · The Vig →
Dutching
Splitting one stake across several outcomes so you win the same amount whichever of them comes in.

E

Edge
The gap between a bet's true probability of winning and the probability its price implies. A positive edge is what makes a bet worth placing.
Lesson 04 · +EV →
EV (expected value)
What a bet is worth on average if you made it over and over. Positive EV (+EV) makes money long-term; negative EV (−EV) loses.
Lesson 04 · +EV →

F

Favorite
The side the market judges more likely to win. It carries minus odds — you risk more to win the same amount.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Fractional odds
The traditional UK odds format, written as a fraction like 5/1 — winnings over stake. Mostly UK books and horse racing.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Futures
A bet on a season-long outcome decided well down the road — a team to win the championship, a player to win MVP.
Futures deep dive →

H

Handle
The total amount of money wagered — on a single market, or by a sportsbook over a period.
Hedge
Placing a bet on the opposite side of one you already have, sized to lock in a guaranteed result instead of leaving it to chance.
Hedge Calculator →
Hold
The share of all money wagered that a sportsbook actually keeps over the long run. Closely related to the vig, but measured against total handle — so it's always a touch lower.
Lesson 03 · The Vig →

I

Implied probability
The win chance baked into a price, written as a percentage. It still includes the book's fee until you devig the market.
Lesson 02 · Implied Probability →
In-play betting (live betting)
Betting on a game after it has already started, with odds that update in real time as the game unfolds.

J

Juice
Another word for the vig — the sportsbook's built-in cut on every bet.
Lesson 03 · The Vig →

K

Kelly criterion
A formula for how much of your bankroll to put on a bet — sized to your edge — so your money grows as fast as possible without risking ruin.
Lesson 05 · Kelly Sizing →

L

Limit
The maximum a sportsbook will let you bet on a given market. Books cut the limits of bettors who consistently win.
Line
The odds or number a sportsbook is offering on a bet — the price, the spread, or the total.

M

Middle
Betting both sides of a market at different numbers, so that if the result lands in the gap between them, both bets win.
Middles deep dive →
Moneyline
The simplest bet there is: pick which team wins the game, straight up — no spread, no total.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →

O

Odds
The price of a bet — it sets what you'd win for what you risk, and reveals the chance the book is giving that outcome.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Over / Under (the total)
A bet on whether the combined score of a game lands above ("over") or below ("under") a number the book sets — that number is the total.

P

Parlay
A single ticket combining several bets ("legs"). Every leg must win or the whole ticket loses — and the vig compounds with each leg you add.
Parlays & SGPs →
Prop bet
A bet on a number a player or team produces — points, yards, strikeouts — rather than on who wins the game.
Prop Bets lesson →
Push
A tie between the result and the line — for example, a team winning by exactly the spread. The bet is cancelled and your stake refunded.

S

Sharp
A consistently winning, professional-level bettor. Also used for a sportsbook that prices accurately and welcomes winners (Pinnacle, Circa).
SGP (same-game parlay)
A parlay built entirely from legs of one game. Books price these with extra markup, which makes them harder to beat than a normal parlay.
Parlays & SGPs →
Spread (point spread)
A handicap the book applies to the favorite — points it must win by — so that betting either side is roughly an even-money proposition.
Square
A casual, recreational bettor — the opposite of a sharp. Books make most of their money from squares.
Stake
The amount of money you put on a bet.
Steam
Fast, sharp movement of a line as heavy or informed money pours onto one side.

U

Underdog
The side the market judges less likely to win. It carries plus odds — a bigger payout for taking the risk.
Lesson 01 · Reading the Odds →
Unit
A standard bet size — often around 1% of your bankroll — used to track results consistently no matter how big the bankroll grows.

V

Vig (vigorish)
The sportsbook's built-in cut, shaded into every price so the two sides of a market add up to more than 100%. The reason most bettors slowly lose.
Lesson 03 · The Vig →
