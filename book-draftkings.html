About this book

DraftKings is one of the two giants in legal U.S. sports betting alongside FanDuel. They started as a daily fantasy company in 2012 and have grown into a full-stack sportsbook operating in roughly two dozen states. Their core audience is recreational, which means their lines are usually a touch softer than sharp markets like Pinnacle or Bookmaker, and they're more welcoming to casual action.

Where DraftKings shines:

  • Mobile-first app that's genuinely well-designed
  • Wide variety of bet types — props, same-game parlays, futures, every gimmick imaginable
  • Heavy promotional calendar (boosts, parlay insurance, no-sweat bets)
  • Generous and frequent welcome offers for new users

Where it doesn't:

  • Lines can drift from sharp prices — sometimes in your favor, sometimes not
  • They will limit sharp bettors who consistently beat them
  • Bonus bet terms can be confusing if you don't read them carefully

If you're new to legal sports betting and want a polished experience to start, DraftKings is a fine first book. Just don't expect them to stay your only book once you're tracking edges across multiple sportsbooks.

What you'll likely get

DraftKings' new-user welcome offer is the kind that pays out in bonus bets — non-withdrawable credits you have to wager before any profit becomes real cash. The most common structure for the past couple of years has been:

Bet $5 → Get $200 in bonus bets (regardless of whether your $5 wins).

That exact dollar amount changes by state, by season, and by promo cycle — but the structure stays roughly the same: small qualifying bet → meaningful bonus-bet payout. Read the fine print on the actual signup page; numbers can vary.

What to know about bonus bets:

  • They're non-withdrawable — you have to bet them
  • If a bonus bet wins, only the profit is withdrawable cash (the bonus stake is consumed)
  • Most have 1× rollover (just bet them once) — but read the terms
  • They expire (typically 7-14 days)

That second point is what most people miss. A $200 bonus bet at −110 wins you $182 in cash, not $382. So the trick isn't placing the bonus bet — it's converting its face value into withdrawable money efficiently.

How to convert it

Bonus bets are most efficiently converted on long-odds bets where you also hedge the other side at a second sportsbook. The math works out to roughly 70-75% of the bonus bet's face value as real, withdrawable cash. Quick walkthrough:

  1. Place your $5 qualifying bet — anything; it doesn't matter much
  2. Wait for the $200 in bonus bets to land in your account
  3. Find a long-odds bet on DraftKings (e.g. +400 or higher)
  4. Use the Bonus Bet Converter to calculate the exact hedge amount
  5. Hedge the opposite side on a second sportsbook at the calculated stake
  6. Whichever side wins, you walk away with ~70% of $200 in real cash — guaranteed
