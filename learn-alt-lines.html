Concept · Equivalent Markets

Same Bet, Two Prices — at the Same Book

"Over 0.5 hits" and "1+ hits" are the exact same bet. The same site lists them as separate markets — and prices them differently. Always check both when you can.

The core inefficiency

Open BetMGM. Pull up Joe Batter's player props for tonight's game. Look at the hits market.

You'll often see this:

Joe Batter — same game, same book, same exact outcome "Over 0.5 hits" → −110 (O/U market)
"1+ hits (To record a hit)" → −135 (Yes/No milestone market)
────────────────────────────
Same outcome: Joe gets at least one hit.
Same book: BetMGM.
Different markets: 25¢ price difference.

"Over 0.5 hits" and "1+ hits" are mathematically identical bets. Joe gets a hit, both win. Joe goes 0-for-4, both lose. There is no scenario where these two tickets settle differently.

But they're priced 25 cents apart. At the same book. Pick the cheaper one. Bet that one. Ignore the other.

This isn't a fluke or a stale line. It happens every day, on most player props, at most US books — and BetMGM is by far the most consistent offender.

Why the same outcome gets two prices

The bet is identical to you. To the book, it's two different products living in two different parts of the sportsbook:

  • The Over/Under market ("Over 0.5 hits", "Under 0.5 hits") is part of the prop ladder — same family as Over 1.5, Over 2.5, etc. It gets priced by whatever model generates the full distribution.
  • The Yes/No milestone market ("To record a hit", "1+ hits", "Anytime TD scorer") is its own market with its own price feed — often priced separately, sometimes by a different model, sometimes by a human trader who tier-prices it by feel.

Two markets, two pricing engines, two sets of vig. They're supposed to converge — and on the sharpest books they roughly do — but the convergence is loose. A trader updating one market doesn't always update the other. A model recalibration on the O/U side doesn't always flow to the milestone side.

Result: the same outcome sits at two different prices, on the same site, for hours at a time.

Common equivalent pairs to check

Anywhere a player prop has a "1+" or "anytime" version and an Over/Under 0.5 version, you have a comparison to make. The big ones:

MLB Over 0.5 hits ≡ 1+ hits / "To record a hit"
Over 0.5 home runs ≡ Anytime HR
Over 0.5 stolen bases ≡ To record a stolen base
Over 0.5 RBIs ≡ 1+ RBI / To drive in a run
Over 0.5 strikeouts ≡ 1+ Ks (pitcher)
NFL Over 0.5 receiving TDs ≡ Anytime TD (receiving)
Over 0.5 rushing TDs ≡ Anytime TD (rushing)
Over 0.5 receptions ≡ To record a reception
Over 0.5 sacks ≡ Anytime sack
NBA Over 0.5 made threes ≡ 1+ three-pointers made
Over 0.5 steals ≡ To record a steal
Over 0.5 blocks ≡ To record a block

Same logic stacks at higher thresholds: Over 1.5 hits ≡ 2+ hits. Over 2.5 hits ≡ 3+ hits. Every book that offers both a ladder and milestone version of a market is a candidate for this check.

The 30-second workflow

You don't need a model. You don't need the sharp consensus line. You don't even need a calculator (though it helps).

Step 1. You've decided you like Joe Batter to get a hit tonight.

Step 2. Open the player-prop page on the book you're using. Find the O/U 0.5 hits price.

Step 3. Scroll to (or search for) the "1+ hits" / "To record a hit" market. Compare.

Step 4. Bet whichever one is cheaper. Done.

The takeaway: always check both when you can. Same book, same outcome, two prices — pick the cheaper one. You're not getting better at picking, you've already picked. You're just refusing to pay 25¢ extra for a ticket that settles exactly the same. Over hundreds of bets, that's the difference between break-even and clearly profitable.

If you want to be more rigorous, plug both into the odds calculator: convert both to implied probability, see which one bakes in less vig, take the cheaper side. Same answer, just with the math written down.

Why BetMGM specifically

BetMGM is the book where this gap shows up most consistently. A few reasons:

  • Their O/U ladders and milestone markets are priced by separate systems. The two prices for the same outcome can drift several cents apart and stay there.
  • Their milestone market ("To record a hit", "Anytime TD") often runs heavier vig than their O/U 0.5. Recreational bettors gravitate to "1+" / "Anytime" because the language sounds simpler — books juice it because that's where the soft money is.
  • Updates don't sync. Lineup news might tighten the O/U side instantly while the milestone side lingers, or vice versa.
  • Their player-prop menus are deep. Lots of equivalent pairs to compare across hits, totals, RBIs, HRs, Ks — every prop is a candidate.

This isn't a knock on BetMGM as a book. They pay out, the app works, they're fine to use. It's just how their pricing is structured. Always check both versions of the bet there before you click.

Where else this shows up (same-book, every book)

This is a same-book phenomenon, not a "shop across books" one. Every major US book has the gap between its O/U ladder and its milestone market — the only thing that varies is how wide:

  • BetMGM — widest gaps, most consistently. Always check both.
  • Caesars — same pattern, slightly less aggressive. Worth checking on every prop.
  • FanDuel — generally tighter; the gap is smaller but still present on softer markets (player threes, stolen bases, etc.).
  • DraftKings — the two sides usually agree within a few cents. Less juice, but still worth a glance.
  • Hard Rock, Fanatics, ESPN BET, Bet365 — newer books, looser sync between markets. Sometimes giant gaps on lower-volume sports.

The bet you want is on the menu twice. One copy is more expensive than the other. That's it. That's the entire edge.

Caveats & risks

  • Books notice price-shopping. If every bet you place on a given book is the cheaper of two equivalent markets, you'll see limits tighten. Mix your action — also bet some main lines, some popular markets, some dogs.
  • Read the rules. Occasionally "1+ hits" and "Over 0.5 hits" do not grade identically — e.g., one might void on a player not appearing while the other settles as a loss. Rare, but check the market rules on each book.
  • Variance on alt-ladder rungs is brutal. An Over 2.5 hits bet at +480 might be +EV vs +500 fair — but you'll lose 80% of these tickets. Use Kelly on the actual edge, not flat units.
  • "Boosted" milestone markets aren't always +EV. A boost from −135 to +110 on "1+ hits" still might be worse than the un-juiced Over 0.5 sitting next to it. Always check the equivalent before you take a boost.
The whole lesson in one line
Always check both when you can.
