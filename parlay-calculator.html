Why parlays are usually a losing play. Every leg has the book's vig built in. When you parlay legs together, that vig compounds multiplicatively — a 4-leg parlay of −110/−110 lines has roughly 10% built-in book edge versus 4.76% on a single bet. The more legs you stack, the worse the math gets.

To make a parlay actually +EV, you need an edge on every single leg that beats the vig you're paying through them. One unsharp leg drags the whole ticket below break-even. That's why sharp bettors place straights, not parlays — except when correlation in same-game parlays creates a real mispricing the books haven't caught.

Plug in fair (no-vig) odds for each leg above to see whether your specific parlay is +EV or just feels exciting.

How parlay odds work: Combined decimal odds = product of each leg's decimal. Combined probability = product of each leg's probability. EV = (true combined prob × combined decimal) − 1. Need fair odds for any leg? Strip the vig with the Devig Calculator.
How to use this

Calculate parlay odds and EV

  1. Enter the American odds for each leg in your parlay. The default is two legs at −110 — add or remove as needed (2-10 legs).
  2. Optionally enter fair (no-vig) odds for each leg. Without these, the calculator returns combined odds and payout but can't compute true EV.
  3. Set your stake. The stats row updates in real time: combined odds, implied probability, true probability, EV %, and the payout if all legs hit.
  4. Read the EV %: positive means the parlay clears the compounded vig (rare); negative means it doesn't (common).
Why this version helps: Most parlay calculators just multiply odds and call it done. We surface the compounded vig via the implied-vs-true probability gap, so you can see — at a glance — how each added leg makes the math worse. The whole point of the tool is to talk you out of bad parlays as much as confirm good ones.

Fair odds for each leg: use the Devig Calculator to strip vig from each market individually, then plug each leg's no-vig price in here for true EV.
Parlay math assumes leg outcomes are independent — same-game parlays often have correlations that books price into the SGP odds. Use the SGP price the book gives you, not the multiplied straight-line odds.
