Reading the Odds

What −115 and +105 actually mean — and why it's the foundation of every bet you'll make.

What is it?

Open any sportsbook and you'll see lines like:

A typical moneyline matchup Mavericks −115
Spurs +105

That's a moneyline bet — the simplest possible bet in sports. No spread, no total. Just: who wins?

The number next to each team is the price — it tells you how much you risk and how much you'd win. Once you can read a moneyline, you can read every other bet on the board: spreads, totals, props, parlays. They all use the same convention.

Reading minus odds (favorites)

A minus sign means you're betting on the favorite — the side the market thinks is more likely to win. The number tells you how much you have to risk to win $100.

Reading minus odds −110 = risk $110 to win $100
−150 = risk $150 to win $100
−300 = risk $300 to win $100 (heavy favorite)

The bigger the minus number, the bigger the favorite — and the more you have to put up to win the same $100. Whatever you risk, you also get back if you win, so a winning $110 bet at −110 returns $210 total ($110 stake + $100 profit).

Of course, most bettors don't bet exactly $110. Here's what those same odds pay on a flat $100 bet:

Same odds, $100 bet instead $100 at −110 = win $90.91 (total payout $190.91)
$100 at −150 = win $66.67 (total payout $166.67)
$100 at −300 = win $33.33 (total payout $133.33)

Same line, smaller payout. The math doesn't care what you bet — it scales with whatever stake you choose.

Reading plus odds (underdogs)

A plus sign means you're betting on the underdog — the side the market thinks is less likely to win. The number tells you how much you'd win on a $100 bet.

Reading plus odds +110 = risk $100 to win $110
+200 = risk $100 to win $200
+400 = risk $100 to win $400 (heavy underdog)

The bigger the plus number, the bigger the underdog — and the bigger the payout if it hits. A winning $100 bet at +200 returns $300 total ($100 stake + $200 profit).

And if you'd rather aim for a flat $100 win instead, you bet less:

Same odds, but to win $100 Bet $90.91 at +110 to win $100
Bet $50.00 at +200 to win $100
Bet $25.00 at +400 to win $100

Bigger underdog, smaller bet for the same $100 payout. Same logic as the favorites — just running in the other direction.

Why it matters more than you think

Almost every other bet you'll see uses these same rules. Spread bets, totals (over/under), player props, futures — they're all priced in the same +/− format. Once you can read a moneyline, you can read every line on a sportsbook's board.

Sharp bettors stop thinking about "$X to win $Y" pretty quickly. Instead, they translate odds into implied probability — what win-rate the price is asking you to beat. A line of −115 says you have to win 53.5% of the time to break even. A line of +105 says you only have to win 48.8% of the time.

That conversion — from price to probability — is where the next lesson begins. Because every line on the board is priced with a tax baked in, and that tax is the reason most bettors lose.
