Welcome Offers & Bonus Bets

Every major US sportsbook gives away hundreds of dollars in welcome offers and ongoing promos. Most new users either give it back to the book trying to "win" with the bonus, or never figure out the conversion math at all. Here's what's actually on the table, and why claiming it correctly is the easiest profit available to anyone with a bankroll.

What is a welcome offer?

Every legal U.S. sportsbook offers a sign-up bonus to new users. They pay real money for it — customer acquisition in this industry is brutally expensive, so they're willing to spend hundreds of dollars per signup to get you in the door.

Common forms:

  • "Bet $5, get $200 in bonus bets." Place a small qualifying bet, get a fixed amount of bonus bets regardless of whether the qualifier wins or loses.
  • "First bet up to $1,000 risk-free." Your first bet is refunded as bonus bets if it loses.
  • "100% deposit match up to $X." Whatever you deposit, the book matches in bonus bets.

Exact terms vary by book, by state, and by promo cycle — but the structure is similar everywhere. Your job is to claim them efficiently.

What are bonus bets?

Bonus bets are the currency these welcome offers pay out in. They are not cash. They're betting credits with three properties that matter:

  • Non-withdrawable. You can't cash them out. You have to bet them.
  • Stake-not-returned. If you bet a $200 bonus bet at −110 and win, you collect $182 in profit. The $200 stake is consumed by the wager.
  • Time-limited. Typically 7-14 days to use before they expire.

Most welcome offers have 1× rollover — you only have to wager the bonus once. Some books require higher rollover (2× or more). Always read the terms before depositing.

Why they're worth claiming

If you placed a $200 bonus bet at −110 and lost, you risked $0 of your own money. If it won, you walked away with $182 in cash. Just placing them blindly is +EV.

But sharps don't place them blindly. They convert them. By placing the bonus bet on one side and hedging the other side at a second sportsbook with real cash, you can lock in roughly 70-75% of the bonus bet's face value as guaranteed cash — regardless of which side wins.

Approximate conversion math Bonus bet face value: $200
Conversion ratio: ~73%
────────────────────────
Guaranteed cash extracted: ~$146

Run that across 6-8 sportsbook welcome offers and you're looking at $1,500-$2,500 in real, withdrawable cash for a few hours of work. No betting knowledge required — just math and accounts at two sportsbooks.

What sharps do

They claim every welcome offer they qualify for. They convert each one methodically. The strategy has a name in the betting world: matched betting — and there are bettors who do only this and never bet a real game in their life.

Whether you eventually want to bet seriously or just collect every promo dollar the books will give you, the playbook starts the same way: open the books, claim the offers, convert the bonus bets to cash. The next four steps walk you through it.

Why this actually works (in plain English)

The whole play in 60 seconds — first as a story, then as the actual math. Same thing told two ways.

☕ THE COFFEE SHOP SCRATCH-OFF

You walk into a coffee shop. The cashier hands you a free scratch-off card. "Welcome — if you scratch off three matching symbols, you win $50. Most don't, but hey, it's free."

You'd love $50, but you know how scratch-offs work — they almost never hit. Most people just scratch, lose, and walk out with nothing.

You're smarter. Before you scratch, you spot Tom X, a regular at the corner table. Tom's seen this promo a thousand times. You walk over and say:

"Tom — I'll give you $42 right now. If my card loses, you pay me $50. If it wins, you keep the $42."

Tom thinks: "Most cards lose. I'll pay $50 occasionally, but I keep $42 the rest of the time. Decent deal." He agrees.

You hand Tom $42 and scratch the card.

  • Card loses (likely) → Tom pays you $50. You gave him $42. Net: +$8 in your pocket.
  • Card wins (rare) → You collect $50 from the cashier. Tom keeps the $42. Net: +$8 in your pocket.

Either way, you walk out with $8 cash. Guaranteed. The $42 to Tom was just parked — it always comes back, one way or another. The free scratch-off was the only thing you actually risked, and it cost you nothing.

Map it to the actual play:

  • Coffee shop = FanDuel (the sportsbook giving you free credits)
  • Free scratch-off card = your $25 / $50 / $200 bonus bet credit
  • Tom X at the corner table = ProphetX (the exchange where regular people take the other side of bets)
  • The $42 you give Tom X = your hedge stake on ProphetX (real cash, but it comes back)
  • The $8 you walk away with = your locked-in cash conversion

Now the actual math

You bet $10 of your FanDuel free credits on a long-odds bet at +500. (Example: a hockey defenseman to score a goal — pays $50 if it hits.)

You bet $41.67 of real cash on ProphetX on the OPPOSITE outcome at -500. (Same player NOT scoring — much more likely. Pays $8.33 if it hits.)

The two outcomes Player scores (rare): FanDuel pays +$50 (bonus consumed) ProphetX hedge loses −$41.67 Net: +$8.33 cash Player doesn't score (common): FanDuel bonus loses $0 (wasn't your money) ProphetX hedge pays +$8.33 winnings + $41.67 stake back Net: +$8.33 cash

Whichever happens, $8.33 in real, withdrawable cash hits one of your accounts. That's 83% of your $10 free credit, converted from non-cash credit into real money — guaranteed, regardless of the game's outcome.

The coffee shop scratch-off and the math table are the same play. Tom X is ProphetX. The free card is your FanDuel bonus credit. The story and the math arrive at the same $8.

Why we recommend FanDuel + ProphetX as the pair

The two-book setup matters. Pick the wrong combo and the math still works, but you'll either struggle with intimidatingly large bet sizes or lose money to vig. Here's why these two specifically:

FanDuel as the bonus book

A bunch of sportsbooks offer free bet credits. FanDuel's is the friendliest for someone just starting out, for two reasons:

  1. FanDuel gives you more. $200 in free bet credits, vs. $100 at DraftKings. Same $5 to unlock.
  2. FanDuel lets you bet any amount you want from your credits — $5, $10, $25, whatever. So you can start with a tiny $10 first attempt, see the whole process work, then size up once you trust it. DraftKings is more rigid: four separate $25 credits, each used at the full $25. That means your matching bet on the other side has to be around $100 right out of the gate — way more nerve-wracking for a first try.

In plain terms: FanDuel lets you dip your toe in. DraftKings makes you jump.

ProphetX as the matching book

ProphetX isn't a regular sportsbook. It's a "betting exchange" — basically a website where you bet against other regular people instead of betting against a sportsbook company. Like a sports stock market.

Why this matters: regular sportsbooks build a small fee into the odds (called "the vig"). That fee eats into your conversion. ProphetX charges almost nothing because there's no sportsbook in the middle. That means more of FanDuel's free money ends up in your pocket.

In real dollars: a $10 free-credit bet on FanDuel converts to ~$8.30 cash when you use ProphetX for the matching bet, vs. ~$7.70 cash if you used a regular sportsbook like DraftKings. Across the full $200 FanDuel bonus, that adds up to about $10-$12 extra dollars in your account — for the same amount of effort.

One catch: ProphetX is currently available in CO, IN, NJ, OH, TN, WY, KY, MA, NC. If your state isn't on that list, use DraftKings as your matching-bet site instead — slightly less efficient, but the entire process still works the same way.

Bet types that work, by sport

The bet shapes below consistently show up on FanDuel between +400 and +700 AND have matching opposite-side bets available on ProphetX. One rule: stick to "main" markets — the standard moneyline, standard spread, standard total, and standard "did this player do thing X at least once" props (the "0.5" line). These have lots of action on both sides. Alt markets — alternate spreads, alternate totals, alternate run lines — often don't have anyone on the other side at ProphetX, which means no matching bet, which means no conversion.

⚾ MLB (baseball)
Apr - Oct · games every day
  • "Player to hit a home run" — pick a hitter at the bottom of the lineup or a backup catcher. Their odds are usually +400-700 because most players don't hit a homer in a given game.
  • Big underdog team's moneyline — when a really bad team plays a really good team, the bad team can be at +300-500 to just win the game outright.
  • "Player to hit 2+ home runs" — much rarer, so odds are +1000 or higher. Only works if ProphetX has a matching bet available.
🏀 NBA (basketball)
Oct - Jun · games every day
  • Big underdog team's moneyline — bad teams playing good teams. Often at +400-700 to just win outright.
  • "Player to record 1+ block" or "1+ steal" — pick a player who doesn't usually defend (guards, shooters). Odds will be +400-700 because those stats aren't their job.
🏈 NFL (football)
Sep - Jan · Sundays + a few other days
  • "Anytime TD scorer" — pick a player who doesn't usually score: an offensive lineman, a backup running back, a tight end. Odds usually +400-900. NFL has the best ProphetX matching availability for these.
  • Big underdog team's moneyline — in lopsided NFL matchups, +400-700 on the heavy dog.
  • "Player to score 2+ TDs" — long odds (+800+), works when ProphetX has the matching bet.
🏒 NHL (hockey)
Oct - Jun · games every day
  • "Anytime goal scorer" — pick a defenseman or a 4th-line forward. Odds are usually +400-900 because they don't score much. Common matching bet on ProphetX.
  • Big underdog team's moneyline — common in NHL, often +300-500.
  • "Player to score 2+ goals" — long odds even for stars (+1000+), good ProphetX availability for top players.
🌐 Whatever's playing
Year-round · something's always on
  • Big underdog moneylines — they exist in every sport, every night there are mismatch games happening somewhere.
  • "Player to do thing X at least once" — the most reliable shape across all sports. Pick a player who rarely does the thing, their odds will be long.
  • Avoid getting fancy with "alternate" lines as a beginner — stick to the basic versions of each bet.
