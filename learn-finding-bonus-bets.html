Finding Bonus Conversion Plays

The conversion math is the easy part. The actual work is finding a long-odds bet on the book where the bonus lives, with a clean opposite-side hedge available on a second book. Three honest paths to find that pair — pay for ease, or learn to spot it yourself.

What you're actually looking for

You just signed up at DraftKings, placed your $5 qualifier, and four $25 bonus bets are now sitting in your account. The Bonus Converter tells you the math, but it needs two inputs you don't have yet: the long-odds bet to place the bonus on, and the hedge price on a second book.

The criteria are simple. You want:

  • Long-odds bet on the bonus book — typically +400 to +900. Longer odds mean more cash if it wins (bonus bets only pay profit, not stake), and a smaller hedge stake to lock in the conversion.
  • Opposite side priced fairly on a second book — clean juice, ideally a sharp book or exchange (ProphetX, Pinnacle if available, or one of the major recreational books offering close to fair). Every cent of vig on the hedge side is a cent of conversion lost.
  • Liquid, settled market — pre-game spreads, totals, or moneylines on major sports. Avoid props, parlays, futures, or in-play for bonus conversion.

The strongest markets for this: NFL spreads and totals at the +400+ end, NBA totals on big numbers, MLB run lines on heavy dogs, NHL puck lines on +200+ dogs, soccer 3-way moneylines with a clear underdog. These are deep, well-priced markets where both sides hit fair-ish vig.

What to skip: parlays (you'll never find a clean hedge), futures (closed liquidity, books shade these aggressively), player props outside the main markets (heavy vig on the other side, often unavailable on the hedge book at all). Stick to game lines.

Once you know what you're looking for, the question becomes how fast you can scan for it. Three paths.

You don't even need the bonus converter tool to find these — you can eyeball them yourself using Pick the Odds' free side-by-side odds view. This is the slower path, but it builds real market intuition that pays off for years when you move on to +EV betting and arb hunting.

How to do it:

  1. Open the Pick the Odds free tier in one tab — it shows every book's line side-by-side on most US markets.
  2. Pick a sport active tonight. Open the game lines board (moneylines + spreads + totals).
  3. Look down the FanDuel column for moneyline underdogs priced at +400 or higher.
  4. For each candidate: check the opposite side's price on ProphetX (the column right next to FanDuel on PTO if PX is enabled, otherwise on your hedge book of choice). If the matching price is in the -400 to -600 range with reasonable juice, you have a pair.

  6. Bookmark candidates and pick the cleanest one. Run it through the Bonus Converter to size the matching bet, then place both legs.

Connecting the dots

Whichever path you use to find the candidate, the rest of the flow is the same:

  1. Find the pair — long-odds bet on the bonus book + clean hedge on a second book. (You just learned three ways.)
  2. Run the Bonus Converter — enter the bonus bet's face value, the long-odds, and the hedge odds. The tool returns the exact hedge stake and the locked-in cash conversion.
  3. Place both legs — bonus bet on the bonus book (using the credit, not cash), hedge stake on the second book (cash). The order doesn't matter much; just don't forget the hedge.
  4. Track it in your Bet Tracker (or eventually Pikkit) — tag conversions as a separate category so they don't pollute your +EV ROI math later.
  5. Repeat for each bonus bet in your stack. By the time you've done 4-6 of these, the pattern recognition takes over and you'll be spotting candidates fast even without a scanner.

Bonus conversion is the most mechanical play in sports betting. Once you've found the right pair, the math is fixed — you're not predicting outcomes, you're locking in a known conversion percentage. The skill is entirely in the find step, which is exactly what this lesson is about.

Whichever tool you use, understand the math underneath. Tools that flag plays for you are great until they're wrong, missing data, or unavailable. The bettor who knows what they're looking at survives every tool change.
