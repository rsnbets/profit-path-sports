How to use the pitcher board

  1. Pick your window. Season is the biggest sample and the most trustworthy baseline. L15 / L10 / L5 show you the recent form — useful for spotting a guy whose velo or command has gone sideways, but remember five starts is a tiny sample.
  2. vs Opp narrows every row to that starter's history against the team he's facing tonight — this season, or last season with the second button. Handy for the "he always gets lit up in Coors / never solves this lineup" question, as long as you hold it loosely (see the warning below).
  3. Every number is a per-start average over that window. Outs is the raw prop stat (18 outs = 6 innings); the innings figure sits underneath it.
  4. Click a column to sort. This is how you actually use the board — sort by HR to find the guys serving up souvenirs, by Outs to find who works deep, by Hits for the traffic-heavy starters.
  5. Green and red are league-relative. Green means that number puts him in the best 20% of starters, red the worst 20% — recalculated for whichever window you've picked, so it always means "compared to everyone else pitching right now."
  6. Tap a pitcher for the start-by-start log plus every prop line posted on him tonight — including how often he's cleared that exact line in the window you picked. When a book hangs more than one number on the same market, each is listed with its own clear rate, so you can see what the extra half-strikeout actually costs you.
  7. Compare that hit rate to the price. Fair Odds is the vig-free market consensus. If he's gone over in 8 of 10 but the market has him at a coin flip, that's a question worth asking — and often a question the market has already answered with the matchup.
Two things this board does not know: who he's facing and where. A 5.1-inning average built against the best lineups in baseball means something very different than one built on a soft schedule. Averages are the starting point of the question, not the answer.
🔒 Lock this in A small window flatters and lies in both directions. When L5 and Season disagree sharply, the truth is usually closer to Season — recent form is real but it's mostly noise, and the market has already seen the same five starts you have. Use L5 to generate questions, use Season to answer them.

That goes double for vs Opp. Two or three starts against one club is the smallest sample on this page, the roster he faced has already turned over, and "owns this team" is the single most over-told story in baseball betting. It's a prompt to go look at why — a park, a lineup full of same-handed bats — never a reason on its own. Rows with fewer than three starts aren't colour-graded here for exactly that reason.
