★ Key Point — Arbing Gets You Limited
An arb is real, guaranteed money — but it's also the fastest way to get your account limited. Books profile hard for it: betting both sides of a market, odd stake sizes, action only when there's an edge. They see the pattern and cut your max bet to pocket change. So vary your stakes, skip the thinnest arbs, and don't hammer the same book — a limited account can't make +EV bets later, either. And the arb has to be live when both bets land: if one line moves before you click the second, the lock is gone. New here? The Arbitrage deep dive covers it.

How to use this

What it does. When two sportsbooks price opposite sides of the same market so that the two implied probabilities add up to less than 100%, betting both sides locks a guaranteed profit no matter who wins. This calculator does the stake math so the profit lands equal either way.

How to use it.

  • Enter the odds for each side — from different sportsbooks (American or decimal).
  • Enter your total stake across the two bets.
  • If the status bar says an arb exists, read the Optimal Split — or the Rounded version for whole-dollar stakes that are faster to place.
  • Place both sides quickly, before either line moves.

Already placed one side? Use the second panel — pick which book you've already bet, enter your stake, and it returns the exact counter-stake to lock the arb (with a rounded option).

Why rounded stakes matter. Most arb calculators hand you stakes like $34.27 / $65.73 — fine on paper, awkward at the betslip. We show both the exact optimal split and a clean whole-dollar version, so you can place real bets fast without rounding by hand.

The real catch. Finding arbs is the easy part. The hard parts: getting both bets down before a line moves, and not getting limited. Arbing is one of the most heavily-profiled patterns in betting — see the Key Point above, and the Arbitrage deep dive, before you make it a habit.
