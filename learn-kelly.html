Kelly Sizing

How much of your bankroll to risk on every bet.

What is it?

You found a +EV bet. Now: how much of your bankroll should you risk on it?

Bet too little and you leave money on the table. Bet too much and a normal cold streak wipes you out — even when every bet was a good one. The Kelly Criterion is the math formula that finds the sweet spot.

It tells you the optimal percentage of your bankroll to risk per bet, given your edge and the odds. The same formula professional bettors, traders, and quant funds use to size positions. It's not a rule of thumb — it's an answer to a math problem.

The math behind it

For a bet with 55% true probability at +100 odds (decimal 2.0):

Kelly stake as a percentage of bankroll Kelly% = (decimal odds × true prob − 1) / (decimal odds − 1)
= (2.0 × 0.55 − 1) / (2.0 − 1)
= 0.10 / 1.0
= 10%

You'd risk 10% of your bankroll on this bet. On a $1,000 bankroll, that's a $100 bet. The bigger your edge, the bigger the bet — that's the whole logic.

Why it matters more than you think

Full Kelly is mathematically optimal for long-run growth — but it's brutally volatile. Even with a real edge, a normal bad month at full Kelly can cut your bankroll in half. That's not a tail risk. That's just variance.

Most sharps run fractional Kelly — half-Kelly (5% in the example above) or quarter-Kelly (2.5%). You give up a slice of growth in exchange for far less variance and a much smaller chance of ruin. Quarter-Kelly captures roughly 75% of full-Kelly's long-run growth with about a third of the volatility.

Roughly speaking — for a real edge Full Kelly → max growth, max swings (50%+ drawdowns common)
Half Kelly → ~93% of growth, ~half the swings
Quarter Kelly → ~75% of growth, ~third of the swings

If you've ever blown up a bankroll on a "lock" you sized way too big — there's no version of Kelly that says to do that. There is no "go all-in" in Kelly's book.

What sharps do about it

They never bet flat. A 1% edge gets a small bet. A 5% edge gets a bigger one. A 10% edge gets the full Kelly fraction. Most run quarter or half-Kelly to stay in the game when (not if) variance hits — because the edge they think they have is always a guess, and over-betting an over-estimated edge is how careers end.

The discipline isn't bigger bets when you're confident. It's right-sized bets, every time, regardless of how it feels.
