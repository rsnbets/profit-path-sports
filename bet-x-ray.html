How to use this

What it does. One calculator that runs the full sharp-betting pipeline: devig the market to get the fair price, compute your edge against your book's price, size with Kelly, then sanity-check the result with hit-rate, variance, sensitivity, CLV, and risk of ruin.

Step 1 — Fair Price. Two ways in:

  • Sharp market: enter both sides of a 2-way market from a sharp source (Pinnacle, Circa, or our Consensus Calc). The page strips the vig and outputs the fair % for both sides.
  • My own model: enter your true probability directly (in % or decimal). Skip the devig.

Step 2 — Your Bet. Pick the side you're betting (only relevant in market mode), enter the price your book is offering, optionally tag the book name. Output: probability gap, EV%, and EV per $100.

Step 3 — Kelly Sizing. Set bankroll and Kelly fraction. Half-Kelly is the default — most pros bet quarter or half, never full. Output: recommended stake, full Kelly reference, expected profit, potential loss. Expand the compare table to see all four common fractions side by side.

Step 4 — Reality Check. The most underrated step:

  • Breakeven hit rate: how often you need to win to break even at this price.
  • Margin of safety: how much room your projection has before the bet flips negative.
  • Loss frequency: how often this exact bet will lose. Important for variance expectations — a +EV +400 dog still loses 75% of the time.
  • Long-run profit: where this edge takes you over 100 and 1000 bets.
  • Sensitivity table: what your EV becomes if your true-prob estimate is off by ±2% / ±5%. If a thin edge collapses to negative under a 2% miss, the bet isn't robust.
  • Verdict: plain-English sanity check — strong, solid, marginal, or skip.

Bonus — CLV preview. Enter the price you expect this market to close at. Beating the close (CLV) is the single best long-run indicator that you're actually +EV.

Bonus — Risk of ruin. Estimates probability of a 50% bankroll drawdown over your bet horizon at the recommended stake. Full Kelly produces big drawdowns even when the edge is real — this is why fractional Kelly exists.

Caveats.

  • Garbage in, garbage out. If your true probability is wrong, every downstream number is wrong. Devig from the sharpest market available.
  • Risk of ruin uses a normal-approximation rule of thumb. Real-world variance can be larger on heavy-favorite or long-shot ladders.
  • Sensitivity uses fixed bumps (±2%, ±5%). For very long-shot bets, the relevant model error is bigger — interpret accordingly.
