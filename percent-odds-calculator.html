What does the percentage actually mean?

It's implied probability — the price of the bet expressed as a chance of winning. A side at 45.05% costs you $45.05 of every $100 the ticket pays out, which is exactly +122 in American odds.

  • Under 50% — underdog territory, plus odds. The lower the number, the bigger the payout.
  • Over 50% — favorite territory, minus odds. The higher the number, the more you lay.
  • Decimal odds = 100 ÷ the percentage. 45.05% → 2.22.

Same math as our Implied Probability lesson — prediction markets just put it on the front of the ticket.

Why is the maker's stake different from the "available" amount?

The dollar amount the exchange shows is what you can bet. But this is an exchange — your winnings don't come from a book, they come from the maker's pocket. The maker's stake is exactly the amount you'd win.

Example: $67 available on a side at 45.05%. Take it all and you'd win $81.72 — so the maker had to post $81.72 to create that $67 of availability. On favorite sides it flips: the maker risks less than what's showing, because you're the one laying the bigger number.

Make, don't take — the whole point of an exchange

The two sides of a percent market always add up to more than 100% — that gap is the spread. Takers pay it. Makers collect it.

Instead of tapping the price that's sitting there, post your own offer just inside the queue on the other side. You get the same bet at roughly the maker's odds shown in green — the entire spread between the two sides goes to whoever posts and waits.
