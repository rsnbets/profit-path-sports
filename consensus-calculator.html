How to use this

What it does. You type the price for a 2-way market (over/under, side A/side B) at multiple sportsbooks. The calculator strips the vig from each book independently, then takes a weighted average of the fair probabilities to give you a single consensus fair price. That number is the benchmark you compare every other book's price against to find +EV bets.

How to use it.

  • Type the sportsbook name in the first column.
  • Enter the American odds for both sides (e.g. −110 and −110, or +135 and −165).
  • Set the weight — how much that book contributes to the consensus. Sharper books get higher weights; recreational books lower.
  • Add as many books as you want. Remove any with the × button.

Suggested weights.

  • 5 — Pinnacle, Circa (the sharp benchmarks).
  • 3 — Bet365, FanDuel, DraftKings (high-volume, generally tight).
  • 2 — BetMGM, Caesars, ESPN BET (mainstream US books).
  • 1 — Hard Rock, Fanatics, smaller regionals (looser pricing).

If you don't have Pinnacle or Circa available, use what you have — the consensus is still useful, just less authoritative.

Devig method.

  • Proportional (default) — divides each side's implied probability by the sum of both sides. The standard sharp method, and what Pinnacle's pricing implies. Use this 99% of the time.
  • Additive — splits the total vig equally between both sides. A simpler approach, useful for symmetric markets like spreads. Slightly different result on lopsided markets.

What to do with the output. The consensus price is your "fair" benchmark. Any book pricing the same outcome at a worse implied probability is −EV. Any book pricing it at a better implied probability is +EV — that's the bet. Plug both into the EV calculator to size it.

Caveats.

  • Garbage in, garbage out. If you weight a soft book the same as Pinnacle, your consensus drifts toward that book's bias.
  • Use the same point in time. Stale prices from one book pollute the consensus.
  • This is for 2-way markets. 3-way markets (with draw, e.g. soccer) need different math — coming later.
